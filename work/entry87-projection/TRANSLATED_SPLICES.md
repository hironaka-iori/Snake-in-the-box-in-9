# Replacing an interval and translating the entire suffix

**Theorem.** Take any of the 131 published 191-edge snakes. Keep its ordered prefix fixed and translate its ordered suffix by any cube translation. Replace the interval separating them by a new induced bridge to gain one edge. Any successful replacement must remove at least **51 original internal vertices**. The prefix and suffix here are both nonempty.

For a source with vertices p₀ through p₁₉₁, let the hinge vertices have indices i and j = i+k+1. Retain p₀ through pᵢ and the images under translation of pⱼ through p₁₉₁. Replace the k original internal vertices by k+1 new ones. The new bridge therefore has k+2 edges. Its length parity and the parity of pᵢ and pⱼ force the translation word to have odd Hamming weight. The 256 odd words are all possible translations for this construction.

Every prefix vertex and every translated suffix vertex are nonconsecutive in the new path. Therefore their mutual distances must all be at least two. For each admissible placement, grow a bridge from the prefix hinge. Each new vertex must avoid the fixed vertices and the neighbours of every fixed non-hinge vertex. It must also avoid the closed neighbourhood of every earlier bridge vertex except its immediate predecessor. Once a bridge vertex is adjacent to the far hinge, the next step must enter that hinge. Hamming distance to that hinge is a lower bound on the remaining steps. These conditions retain every valid bridge. The terminal check supplies the final edge. All pairs inside each unchanged arm retain their original distances.

It suffices to test k=50. A replacement with smaller k can be viewed as one with k=50 by moving the hinges outward along the source until the bridge swallows enough retained vertices. One vertex in each outside arm can be preserved since the full source has 192 vertices. Use exactly the same translation and completed path. The added bridge steps are existing consecutive steps of the completed path.

For k=44, there are 131 × 147 × 256 = 4,929,792 placements. Of these, 14,836 pass the complete cross-distance and endpoint-distance filters. Exhausting their bridges takes 1,083,804,170 visited partial paths and produces no 193-vertex witness. A positive control deletes an interior transition of a known snake, then reconstructs a full valid path from the shortened input. All positive outputs are checked against each vertex pair. The exact family preserves the relative orientations of the two arms and allows arbitrary translations. Coordinate permutations of a single arm are a larger family.

At k=50, all 4,728,576 placements were tested. There were 17,448 separated and distance-compatible pairs of hinges. The complete bridge searches visited 31,856,318,868 partial paths and returned no witness.
