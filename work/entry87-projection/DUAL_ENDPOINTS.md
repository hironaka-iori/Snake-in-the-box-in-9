# A second path between the endpoints of every odd-dimensional snake

**Theorem.** Let S be the vertex set of an induced path with at least one edge in an odd-dimensional hypercube. Let Y contain every cube vertex having an odd number of neighbours in S. The graph induced by Y contains a path between the two endpoints of S. Some such path is induced, and every internal vertex of it is outside S.

Write D for the odd-neighbour operator. In the graph induced by S, every internal path vertex has even degree and both endpoints have odd degree. Hence S ∩ D(S) consists of precisely those endpoints. By the [inversion lemma](SYNDROME.md), D(Y)=S. In the graph induced by Y, its odd-degree vertices therefore form Y ∩ D(Y) = Y ∩ S, the same pair of endpoints. Every connected component of a finite graph has an even number of odd-degree vertices. The two endpoints lie in the same component. Take a shortest path between them inside Y. A chord would shorten it, and all of its internal vertices lie in Y outside S. Edges from this second path to unrelated members of the original path can still occur.

The identity has a useful converse for testing changes of a partial construction. For disjoint candidate parity classes E and O, the numbers of odd-degree vertices on their two sides are |E ∩ D(O)| and |O ∩ D(E)|. Every even-length snake satisfies exact counts two and zero, in the appropriate endpoint orientation. In particular every required intersection is easy to update under the changes of syndrome sites: D(S symmetric-difference T) = D(S) symmetric-difference D(T).

For all 131 known 191-edge paths, direct checks give |Y| between 136 and 168. Shortest complementary paths inside Y have between 3 and 23 edges. Forty-six sources have length 3 and forty-eight have length 5. These paths supply exact places at which an alternative connection between the original ends can run, while the original internal vertices remain available for rearrangement. The first construction experiment from the theorem adds **every vertex on any shortest auxiliary path** to each entire bank snake. A vertex v lies on a shortest route between a and b exactly when d(a,v)+d(v,b)=d(a,b) in the graph on Y. Allow any selection of 193 vertices and any ordering from the resulting union. The 131 allowed regions have sizes 194 through 243. Each complete induced-degree formula is already contradictory. The 131 trimmed proofs use 1,394,996 resolution steps in total and no generated connectivity cuts. Thus rerouting through these specific shortest corridors fails for **every** bank input, even when the old path can be discarded anywhere. For longer corridors use d(a,v)+d(v,b)≤d(a,b)+k with an even k.
