# Replacing the prevalent 96-vertex middle

Of the 88 stored 191-edge snakes that cross a coordinate twice, 84 share one 96-vertex middle. A regrowth catalogue contains 1,299 inequivalent middles of this size, including that shape. A 192-edge target needs 97 outer vertices in two separate paths. A 29,96,68 or a 46,96,51 split would each suffice. Both root locations and all internal edges of the middle are fixed only after a placement is chosen.

## An unrestricted short arm

Choose which endpoint of a candidate middle receives the long outer arm. Normalize its rooted transition word and try all injections of the used coordinate labels into the eight cube directions. The long arm may share only its root address with the middle. It must also avoid the closed neighbourhood of the other crossing address. From that other address grow an arbitrary induced short arm, avoiding the projected middle and the closed neighbourhood of the long arm. Every path assembled in this way is checked against all vertex pairs. A positive control reconstructs a known 191-edge snake with outer arms of 94 and two vertices.

The long-arm source comprises 196,519 normalized words obtained from the Q8 catalogue and six completed regrowth runs. Each word is a suffix, in either orientation, containing at least 84 vertices. Every prefix of a word down to the stated minimum is allowed. With a long arm of at least 84 vertices, the 1,299 middles yield 195 candidate placements; all 195 use the already-known middle. Lowering the minimum to 64 yields 1,282 candidate placements on 48 middles. None admits a short arm completing 193 vertices. This is an exact exclusion for this long-arm menu and unrestricted short arms. For the 47 alternative middles admitting a placement, we then allowed both arms to vary freely. All 47 fail at both 192 and 193 total vertices. The 192-vertex exclusions also cover every larger target with the same middle, since trimming a free outer endpoint preserves both crossing edges until only the two roots remain. A control with the stored middle reconstructs an all-pairs-checked 191-edge path in this same unrestricted model. The other 1,251 new 96-vertex middles form the next unrestricted completion experiment. ## How much of the old outer arms can survive?

For the 84 sources, the two crossing addresses are at Hamming distance three. Exactly 595 of the 1,299 middle shapes have ends at this distance, including the stored shape. For each of the 594 alternatives, and each of the 84 outer-arm pairs, fix the crossing addresses at 0 and 7. There are 2×3!×5! relative placements, allowing either orientation. This gives 71,850,240 indexed comparisons. On each arm ordered outward from its crossing, the first address shared with the replacement middle forces that vertex and its entire remaining suffix to be removed if the original root prefix is retained.

**At least 64 of the original 96 outer vertices must be trimmed before any of the 594 alternatives can fit.** This lower bound is attained as a prefix-compatibility result. With source entry 38, candidate middles 982 and 983 each permit 32 original outer vertices to be retained, after removing 50 vertices from one free end and 14 from the other. Any full completion still has to rebuild the rest of both arms.
