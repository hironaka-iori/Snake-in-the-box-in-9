# Opposite crossing addresses in the 95-vertex catalogue

Of 4,193 distinct 95-vertex middles in the completed regrowth catalogue, 74 join opposite corners of the eight-cube. To reach 192 edges, a snake with one of these middles needs two outer arms containing 98 vertices altogether. We examined the construction in which one outer arm is the antipodal image of the other. Each arm would then use 49 vertices.

Identify each address x with its antipode x XOR 255. In the quotient graph each of the eight possible coordinate changes gives one distinct neighbour. The starting pair is {0,255}. Discard every pair containing an internal middle vertex, since a mirrored construction would occupy both lifts of every chosen pair. Any successful pair of arms projects to an induced path of 49 vertices starting at the root pair. Repeating a pair would make the arms intersect, and an extra quotient edge would give an extra edge within or between the arms. Conversely a rooted induced path in the remaining quotient lifts to two disjoint, mutually nonadjacent arms. Together with the prescribed middle they yield a snake.

Antipodes in the eight-cube have the same parity. The 49-vertex path requires 25 even and 24 odd pairs in the connected component of the root. This eliminates 63 middles. For each of the other eleven, delete enough vertices of each parity to leave the required counts, always keeping the root. If the component has E edges and the largest degrees among the permitted deletions sum to D, at least E−D edges remain. Even when the selected highest degrees overlap on an edge, this is a valid lower bound. In the eleven cases these bounds range from 50 to 62; an induced path on 49 vertices has exactly 48 edges. Thus all 74 mirrored constructions are excluded by counting.

The unrestricted experiment allowed the two arms to have different shapes and any split of their total length. All 74 middles fail at 191 edges, which requires 97 outer vertices. Removing a free outer tip from any 192-edge completion would give a 191-edge completion with the same middle. Thus all 74 also fail at 192 edges. ## Translating one arm onto the other

The symmetry argument also covers every 95-vertex middle, by translating one arm by the difference of its two crossing addresses. The quotient identifies x with x XOR that difference. Two distinct pairs joined by two edge matchings would lift to a square; such pairs cannot both be occupied. The root component is therefore built using single matchings. The edge lower bound counts both matchings wherever both pairs survive.

All 4,193 catalogue entries fail this equal-arm construction. Parity capacity eliminates 2,580, the edge count eliminates 1,152, and the last 461 fail the maximum-degree constraints with the required parity counts. No connectivity cuts were needed in these 461 decisions. [All decisions](translated-outer-arms-95.json), [encoder and lift](translated_outer_arms.py).

There is an elementary obstruction for any pair of crossing addresses at distance two. If the translation flips coordinates i and j, a step in either coordinate and its translated step occupy all four vertices of a square. Therefore each arm stays in a six-cube with coordinates i and j fixed. Partition this six-cube into four four-cubes. Each contains at most eight vertices of an induced linear forest, giving at most 32 vertices in the arm, short of the required 49. This argument applies to every such middle, including shapes outside the catalogue. [Four-cube lemma](PROJECTION_BALL.md#the-middle-arm-must-have-65-through-99-vertices).
