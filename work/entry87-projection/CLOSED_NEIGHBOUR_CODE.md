# How far a longer snake must move its closed-neighbour signature

For a set S of vertices in Q9, mark a vertex when its closed neighbourhood contains an odd number of members of S. Write T(S) for those marked vertices. The following restrictions hold for a 192-edge target P and any one of the 131 saved 191-edge snakes S, in any relative cube orientation.

1. The two signatures differ at **10 or at least 22** addresses. Their difference has size 2 modulo 4. If there are exactly ten differences, they comprise a whole closed neighbourhood.
2. In that ten-difference case, the paths' vertex sets differ at **at least 21** addresses. At least ten sites of the saved snake must disappear and at least eleven new sites must enter.
3. The signature of P contains its 191 interior vertices and excludes both endpoints. At least 189 marked vertices have degree at least three within the signature.

The first and third assertions follow from cube geometry. The second also uses a complete finite test on the 131 specified source sets. The binary code used here has an established literature, including the self-duality result of Fish, Key and Mwambene in [Binary codes from designs from the reflexive n-cube](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/267146635_Binary_codes_from_designs_from_the_reflexive_n-cube). Below is a self-contained derivation of the low-weight facts needed for the snake application.

### The code and its minimum words

Write addition of subsets for symmetric difference. Let D count open-neighbour incidences modulo two and C=I+D. In odd cube dimension d, D²=I by counting two-step walks, so C²=0. Split the cube into two (d−1)-dimensional slices. With B=I+D on one slice, B²=I and C(a,b)=(u,Bu), where u=Ba+b. Thus the image and kernel of C both consist exactly of the pairs (u,Bu). The symmetric matrix C makes this code self-dual. Every codeword has even weight.

For odd d≥5 the minimum nonzero weight is d+1, and its only words of that weight are the closed stars. Choose the smaller slice S of a nonzero word, of size m. The other slice is the symmetric difference of its m closed balls, each of size d. Each pair of distinct balls shares at most two points. At a site of multiplicity t the parity indicator is at least t−2 binom(t,2), strictly so for t≥3. Consequently the word weighs at least m(d+3−2m). For a word of weight at most d+1, m lies between 1 and (d+1)/2. The bound equals d+1 at both ends and is strictly larger between them. At the upper end m≥3, equality would force all pairs of centres to have distance at most two and no three balls to meet. Any three such centres have a common point in their balls, the coordinatewise majority. Indeed, if their distances from that point are a,b,c, then the three pair distances are a+b,b+c,c+a. A value of two for any one of a,b,c would force the two other centres to coincide. This rules out the upper end. The singleton slice gives exactly one closed star.

### Adding a parity coordinate

Append a parity bit to every (n−1)-bit address, with n even. Cube words now occupy one parity of Qn. The n addresses of a closed star become the n open neighbours of a centre of the other parity. Denote this latter open star by N(v). A set U is a codeword just when each vertex of the other parity has an even number of neighbours in U. This identification follows by puncturing the added bit, which carries the parity check incidence matrix to I+D. In particular the one-parity code in Qn has minimum n, with only stars at that weight, for even n≥6.

Delete any two coordinates. The four sections of U are A00,A01,A10,A11, subsets of the remaining (n−2)-cube. Suppose U has even total address parity. The discrepancies E=A00+A11 and F=A01+A10 lie on its even and odd residual parities. The checks yield

    D A00 = D A11 = F,    D A01 = D A10 = E.

Here D operates on the residual cube. Thus E,F themselves belong to its one-parity codes. The weight of U is |E|+|F| plus twice the number of doubled fibres. A doubled fibre contains both lifts of one residual address.

For n=8 or 10, a nonempty word of weight below 2(n−2) has exactly one nonzero discrepancy for every pair of coordinates. Both nonzero would give weight at least 2(n−2). If both vanish, every occupied fibre is doubled and its lower section satisfies the residual code, again giving at least 2(n−2). With exactly one discrepancy zero, the support is invariant under exactly one of two operations on those coordinates. One swaps them; the other swaps them and complements both. The first swaps 01 with 10 and the second swaps 00 with 11. When exactly one discrepancy vanishes, exactly one operation fixes the set.

Assign sign 0 or 1 to the unique stabilizing swap of each pair. Conjugating the signed swap of j,k by that of i,j gives the signed swap of i,k with the sum of their signs modulo two. Uniqueness forces the signs to have the form b_i+b_j. Translating by b now turns all these symmetries into ordinary coordinate swaps. The translated support is a union of complete Hamming-weight layers on one parity. In n=8 or 10, all layers except weights 0,1,n−1,n have size at least binom(n,2)>2(n−2). The two singletons together weigh at most two. Each of the two one-away layers is a star of size n, and their union already weighs 2n. This proves the gap between n and 2(n−2).

### Words at the second weight

At exactly 2(n−2), a zero pair of discrepancies forces one nonempty doubled residual star. It consists of the difference of two stars whose centres differ in the deleted coordinates: the two cross-fibre neighbours cancel. If every pair instead has exactly one discrepancy, the preceding signed-swap argument rules out this weight. The remaining case has two nonzero discrepancies. Both have minimum weight n−2, hence are stars N(s),N(r) on opposite residual parities, and there are no doubled fibres. For A contained in a residual star N(s), say with t of its m=n−2 sites, D A has size

    (t modulo 2) + t(m−t).

The centre contributes precisely when t is odd. On two-coordinate offsets from s, the contributing pairs have exactly one of their directions chosen. For m=6 or 8 the expression equals m only at t=1,m−1. The equations on the two sections therefore force s,r to be neighbours. Both lower sections are singletons or their complements within their respective stars. The two signed swaps interchange the respective choices independently. Normalize to A00={r} and A01={s}, where r is even. The resulting word is N((r,10))+N((s,11)). This classifies **every** word at the second weight for n=8 and 10. In particular the eight-cube residual words of sizes up to twelve have sizes eight and twelve, with the twelve-site words exactly the differences of two stars at centre distance two.

### Classifying through weight twenty in Q10

For a weight through twenty, a pair with zero discrepancies contributes twice a sum of residual codeword weights. The only positive possibilities are 16 and the doubled-star case above. If every pair has exactly one discrepancy, the complete-layer argument applies. At weight twenty it also allows the union of both ten-site one-away layers, two disjoint stars. To handle a pair with two nonzero discrepancies, the unordered pair of their weights is (8,8) or (8,12). They occupy opposite parities. One eight-site discrepancy can be normalized to N(0) by translating. The other is either N(s) with |s|=1,3,5,7 or N(s)+N(t) with |s+t|=2. Coordinate permutations and exchanging s and t leave seven pair types in the latter case. They are obtained directly by sorting the eight column pairs of their two bit strings.

For each of these eleven configurations, enumerate A00 on the support of E and A01 on the support of F. Outside those supports allow in total at most (20−|E|−|F|)/2 additional addresses, each producing one doubled fibre. With A00,A01 chosen, the other two sections are forced by E,F. The two residual equations displayed above are exact and sufficient. The [complete independent section enumeration](low_weight_lifts.py) finds four weight-16 lifts at centre distance one and four weight-20 lifts at each of centre distances three, five and seven. There are no lifts in the seven (12,8) configurations. Every returned word is explicitly equal to the difference of two stars. An independent [direct enumeration](second_weight.py) verifies all 262,144 assignments in the (8,8) case without doubled fibres. [All eleven results](low-weight-lifts.json), [direct enumeration counts](second-weight-lifts.json).

There is also a short geometric verification of the eleven configurations. First take E=N(s), F=N(r). Since D A00=N(r), the set K=A00+{r} is a residual codeword. It consists of a subset of N(s) and at most three other points, including r. If there are at most two doubled fibres in all, |K|≤11, so K is zero or a single eight-site star. In the latter case K overlaps N(s) in at least five points. Distinct stars intersect at most twice, hence K=N(s). Thus A00 is either {r} or {r}+N(s). The same argument gives A01={s} or {s}+N(r). When r,s are neighbours there are no doubled fibres, giving the sixteen-site word above. Otherwise exactly r and s are doubled, giving twenty sites. The independent signed swaps normalize the two sections to singletons. In both cases the full word is N((r,10))+N((s,11)).

Now take E=N(s)+N(t) of size twelve and F=N(r) of size eight. There are no doubled fibres. The weight formula for D A01 applied within N(r) forces two or six of its eight neighbours to be chosen. Therefore E is the difference of the stars centred at two neighbours of r. The choice of two centres is unique: equality of two such representations puts a nonzero set of at most four centres in the residual kernel unless the pairs are identical. In particular s,t both neighbour r. Their two common neighbours are r and r′. The set K=A00+{r} is a residual codeword of size at most thirteen, contains r, omits r′, and lies within N(s)∪N(t). If K is one star, its centre must be s or t, since any other star meets that union at most four times. Both choices contain r′. If K has twelve sites, it is the difference of two stars. At least one of their centres must be s or t, since otherwise their union meets N(s)∪N(t) in at most eight places. Adding the corresponding star back to K shows that the other star lies entirely within N(s)∪N(t), so its centre is also s or t. Their difference omits r. This excludes the (12,8) case.

Thus every codeword in Q9 of weight at most twenty is zero, a closed star of size ten, or a difference of two closed stars of size sixteen or twenty. The next possible weight congruent to two modulo four after ten is twenty-two. It is attained in the code: choose three Q10 centres of the same parity, each at distance two from the others and with one common neighbour. Their three stars have parity weight 30−12+4=22.

### Applying the classification to paths

For any S, |C S| is 2|S| modulo four in Q9, by the [modulo-four neighbourhood law](SYNDROME_WEIGHT.md). The difference between 192-site and 193-site paths has odd size, so its C-image has weight two modulo four. This gives assertion 1. If C(P+S)=N[v] in Q9, then P+S={v}+K for a kernel word K. The [finite census](star_repairs.cpp) checks every centre v and zero, one or two arbitrary kernel stars for each record. After toggling the kernel stars, any occupied site of degree zero or above two must lie in the closed neighbourhood of the final toggle. Otherwise the defect survives. The implementation also checks the target size, the two endpoints and connectivity. No degree-feasible result survives. This handles 131 source cases with no star, 67,072 with one star, and 17,136,896 pairs of stars, each with all possible final toggles covered. The [independent checker](star_repairs.py) compares exact solution counts on every source subset of Q3 and recovers a deliberate two-star perturbation of a real Q9 bank path. [Bank census and positive control](closed-star-repairs.json). Since all kernel words of size at most 20 are covered, K must have at least 22 sites. Toggling v leaves at least 21 differences, proving assertion 2.

For assertion 3, inside a path the closed-neighbour parity equals one exactly at its degree-two interior. Moreover D(T(P))=T(P) by C²=0, so every marked vertex has odd degree within the marked set. Each interior vertex other than the two immediately beside the endpoints has two interior path neighbours. Its marked degree is consequently at least three. Lean 4.19 checked linearity, D(T)=T, C²=0 and invariance under kernel changes without axioms. [Formal statements](ClosedSignature.lean).

### The next possible signature difference

Every 22-site word is the sum of three stars whose centres are connected by steps of Hamming distance two in Q10. To verify completeness, the residual Q8 code is doubly even: all its generating stars have weight eight and any two generators have even intersection. Therefore the residual pairs at weight 22 are still (8,8) and (8,12). A zero discrepancy in both parities for one coordinate pair gives a weight divisible by eight. If every pair has exactly one nonzero discrepancy, the complete-layer argument rules out 22. In the remaining mixed case the same eleven configurations suffice, now with up to three doubled fibres. The direct [section calculation](low_weight_lifts.py) finds twelve normalized lifts of weight 22, in two configurations. Each is explicitly reconstructed as the sum of three stars with at least two of the three pairwise centre distances equal to two. [All eleven configurations at 22](low-weight-lifts-22.json).

In ordinary Q9 notation these centre pairs have distance one or two. Conversely every connected triple produces weight 22. Pick its middle centre. If the two direction-pairs of the steps overlap, there are three pairwise intersections of size two and one triple intersection. If they are disjoint, there are two pairwise intersections and no triple intersection. The parity weights are 30−12+4 and 30−8 respectively. The representation is unique because two distinct triples would put their symmetric difference, of size at most six, in a kernel of minimum weight ten. There are 512 possible middle centres. Of the pairs among the 45 directions of distance two in Q10, 630 are disjoint and 360 share one coordinate. Each triangle has three possible middle centres. Thus there are exactly **512(630+360/3)=384,000 distinct signatures** of this weight.
