# How a single address can prevent two long faces from joining

Normalize the single crossing of a 191-edge bank snake to the two copies of address zero. Its two rooted eight-dimensional paths have 97 and 95 vertices. Both start and end even in their respective Q8 coordinates. Keep the even addresses on each face. The long face supplies 49 and the short face supplies 48, with zero their only common address. In a 192-edge target with the same even sets, either the long face or the short face must acquire an odd endpoint. These give the 97+94 and 96+95 edge splits, respectively.

**Two-face reconstruction lemma.** Fix the even sets in both faces and specify which face ends odd. In each face form the witness graph from the [one-parity lemma](PARITY_SETS.md), using just the eight coordinates. Enumerate its Hamilton paths starting at zero. On the face that ends odd, the terminal anchor must have a private odd neighbour. For each edge of either ordered graph, list all odd addresses adjacent to exactly those two anchors in that face. Include one additional list for the odd endpoint, consisting of the private neighbours of its anchor. The two face paths can be joined with one crossing exactly when these lists have distinct representatives.

Indeed, each odd vertex between anchors must have exactly those two even neighbours. An odd endpoint must have just its terminal neighbour. Distinct representatives give both within-face paths and keep the two layers disjoint away from their shared even root. Conversely every such snake supplies a choice for every list. Members of two different lists on the same face are already distinct by their anchor incidence. The remaining obstruction is competition for odd addresses between faces.

**All 25 original placements.** The 50 choices of placement and odd-end face give 40 cases where one face has no suitable rooted Hamilton order. In the other ten, both faces can complete in isolation. Sixteen pairs of orders occur. Every pair has the same minimal obstruction from Hall's marriage criterion: two obligations on different faces have just one odd address between them.

For example, in the first bank placement the long face needs a connector between even anchors 29 and 85. Its only allowed address is 21. The short face can gain the extra edge only by finishing from even anchor 5. Its only private odd neighbour is also 21. Selecting both copies produces a second crossing and closes a cycle. In the saved coordinate normalization every one of the sixteen conflicts is concentrated at address 21 or 22. The full report lists both obligations and the address for every order.

**One even replacement shared between the two faces.** Free the complete odd part of each path and allow one original nonzero even anchor, in either face, to be replaced. Its new address may be any even address outside both fixed even sets. There are 128 even addresses and 49+48−1=96 already used. Hence each ordering of the lengths has (48+47)×32=3,040 admissible one-anchor replacements per placement. Across both splits in 25 placements, all 152,000 replacements and all 50 unchanged sets were covered. In 152,029 cases at least one rooted order is impossible. The other 21 give 36 pairs of orders, all incompatible across faces. Thus a construction in these splits needs at least two changes among the two even sets, even with both complete odd sets and both orders free. Each of the 25 positive controls reconstructs and checks a 191-edge arrangement.
