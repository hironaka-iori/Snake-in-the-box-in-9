# Every one-crossing length split near the 25 published placements

**Theorem.** Normalize the root of the crossing and fix the common coordinate frame of any one of the 25 single-crossing placements from the bank. A 192-edge snake crossing that coordinate once at the same address must omit at least six of the original sites on at least one of the two faces. Both paths may change their shape, order, far endpoint, or length.

Write A and B for the two sets of selected addresses in Q8. They both contain zero and are otherwise disjoint. The two faces must contain 193 vertices in total. The known maximum length of a Q8 snake is 98 edges, so both sets have at most 99 vertices. This leaves exactly six ordered size pairs: (94,99), (95,98), (96,97), (97,96), (98,95), (99,94). These statements concern the fixed root and frame of each reference placement.

At each placement, require at most five omissions separately from each reference set. New addresses are unrestricted subject to this count and the path constraints. Each face begins at its root with degree one. Its far endpoint has degree one and the parity dictated by its size. Every other selected vertex has degree two. Enforce alternating-parity vertex counts and exclude all four-cycles. These necessary conditions already contradict the omission limits in all **150** cases. No connectedness cut was needed. The search separately decoded its original 191-edge positive control and checked every pair of vertices.

The six archives contain each concrete formula, its trimmed DRAT proof and its verification receipt. Each received two checker passes at generation. The trimmed proofs use 76,443,990 resolution steps in total. Use `python joint_archive.py --replay /path/to/drat-trim` for independent replay from the archives.

The [odd-address obstruction](JOINT_PARITY.md) explains why both paths competing for one address can block a repair even when each can grow separately. A second experiment went further and substituted one nonzero even anchor on **each** face while freely permuting anchors and reselecting all odd vertices. Each individual side can now claim an even address vacated by the other. Only three disjoint edited pairs admit appropriate rooted orders in both faces, all with the short face ending odd. Their six order pairs all fail the distinct-witness matching. Thus, with at most one even substitution on each side, neither of the two nearest splits works, independently of how many odd addresses change.

### Two replacements on one face

Allow two nonzero even substitutions on either one of the two faces, retaining the other even anchor set and freeing every odd address. There are 100 choices of placement, odd endpoint face and edited face. In 40 choices, the unchanged face has no permitted Hamilton order. The other 60 contain 32,986,480 pairs of removed and added anchors. Of these, 1,021 pass the first degree screen, ten admit rooted Hamilton orders and all sixteen pairs of orders fail the joint matching test. Together with the distributed cases, **a successful repair of either nearest split must replace at least three even anchors in total**, when the two even class sizes and root are fixed as in the bank. [Native two-substitution search](joint_even_edges.cpp), [count and witness checks](joint_even_edges.py), [coverage](joint-even-two-0-25.json).

Allowing two substitutions on *each* face also fails. Independently catalogue every rooted order on each side after one or two even substitutions. New anchors may occupy sites removed from the other side. Then combine complete orders only when their final even sets intersect just at zero. For the ordered replacement counts (2,1) and (2,2), no compatible pair of independently orderable even sets remains. For (1,2), six such pairs survive and their twelve orders are all excluded by the matching test. Together with the smaller counts, this gives a stronger conclusion: **at least one of the two faces must replace three or more of its original even anchors**. All odd addresses remain unrestricted.
