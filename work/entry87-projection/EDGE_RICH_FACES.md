# Counting edge incidences in the eight-cube

Each edge belongs to seven of the sixteen seven-dimensional faces. If an induced linear forest on 117 vertices has at most 13 components, it has at least 104 edges. Some face therefore contains at least 46 internal edges, since 7×104=728 exceeds 16×45=720.

The existing dense-face test excludes face populations 62 through 64, so the remaining splits are 56+61, 57+60 and 58+59. After excluding the 56+61 split, the 46-edge test only needs seven-faces on 57, 58, 59 and 60 vertices. At 59 vertices it reduces 186,864 shapes to 212. A separate census enumerating only forests with at least 46 edges reproduces every representative, orbit size and stabilizer at 59 vertices. At 58 vertices the restricted census yields 256 shapes. The denser six-cube in the recursive enumeration can now have 29 vertices. Orient its seven-face split by occupancy, and classify the 29-vertex six-cube up to all signed coordinate permutations before restoring the true counts. An odd population has no tied coordinate majorities. [Enumerator](near_full_faces.cpp), [seven-cube canonicalizer](seven_face_classes.cpp), [list at 58](rich-46-classes-58.txt), [list at 59](rich-46-classes-59.txt).

There is a second useful way to select a face when the edge target is higher. Suppose every face of an m-vertex forest has between 57 and 60 vertices. Choose the denser side of each coordinate, breaking ties arbitrarily. The other eight faces each have at most 48 edges. If the whole forest has E edges, at least one chosen side has at least ceil(7E/8)−48 edges. At E=109 this forces 48 edges. At m=118 the denser side has 59 or 60 vertices. Only three 59-vertex shapes and six 60-vertex shapes reach 48 edges.

**Every 118-vertex induced linear forest in the eight-cube has at most 108 edges.** To verify this, first rule out populations 61–64 in a hypothetical forest with 109 edges by six template inequalities and the 37 exceptional 61-vertex inequalities. Next check the nine denser-face shapes from the preceding paragraph. Each has an integer bound below 109 with the opposite half free. [Result](edge-window-118-109-dense.json), [certificate archive](edge-window-118-109.weights.gz), [auditor](edge_window.py). Run `python edge_window.py 118 109 --archive edge-window-118-109.weights.gz --replay` to check the integer inequalities from the archive.

The full 117-vertex, 13-component test also applies one edge below 233. A 232-edge nine-cube path has 233 vertices. Some direction changes at most 25 times, so each half contains at most 13 runs. One half has at least 117 vertices, and shortening runs yields exactly 117. Thus excluding this forest would give the **unconditional upper bound of 231 edges** by applying the argument to a 232-edge prefix of any longer candidate.

## All 61-vertex faces are excluded

All 1,630 shapes of a 61-vertex seven-face are now excluded at total population 117 and at least 104 induced edges. Four full-template inequalities cover 555 shapes. Individual fixed-face integer bounds cover 1,016 and checked resolution proofs cover the other 59. This reduces every coordinate cut to **57+60 or 58+59**. The resolution auditor reconstructs the clauses from the vertex set, validates the added cycles, matches the formula and proof hashes, and can run an independent proof checker. [Coverage](edge-window-117-104-facets.json), [integer certificates](edge-window-117-104-facets.weights.gz), [resolution auditor](facet_resolution.py).

## The least-used coordinate at length 233 would change 21 to 25 times

At both 117 and 118 vertices, every eight-cube induced linear forest has at most **106 edges**. For each population, first exclude all seven-faces with 61 or more vertices at 107 edges. Each remaining cut contributes at most 48 edges from its smaller side. Since 7×107=749 exceeds 8×(48+45)=744, one of the eight denser sides has at least 46 edges. The denser side has 59 or 60 vertices. All 212 shapes at 59 and all 147 at 60 have fixed-face bounds below 107, including the correction for the changed population at 118. [117-vertex verification](edge-window-117-107-dense.json), [117-vertex certificates](edge-window-117-107.weights.gz), [118-vertex verification](edge-window-118-107-dense.json), [118-vertex certificates](edge-window-118-107.weights.gz).

It follows that ten paths in an eight-cube occupy at most 116 vertices: a larger forest could be shortened to 117 and would have at least 107 edges. Eleven paths occupy at most 117 vertices by the same argument at 118. A nine-cube snake with at most 20 crossings in a coordinate thus has at most 116+117−1=232 edges, since its runs split at most ten and eleven per half. A 233-edge snake would therefore use **every** coordinate at least 21 times. Averaging still gives some coordinate used at most 25 times.
