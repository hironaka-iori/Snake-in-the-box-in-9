# How many vertices can the two outer paths occupy?

**Two mutually nonadjacent induced paths in one eight-dimensional face occupy at most 112 vertices in total.** Consequently a 192-edge nine-cube snake that crosses a coordinate exactly twice must put at least **81 vertices** in the middle path in the opposite face. For a snake crossing a coordinate exactly three times, each side consists of two such paths, so each side contains between 81 and 112 vertices. These conclusions allow arbitrary roots and endpoints.

The local calculation counts the minimum number of components in an induced linear forest in a seven-cube. Isolated vertices count as components. For 57 through 64 selected vertices the respective minima are **9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15 and 16**. Equivalently, at each of these eight occupancies the maximum number of edges is exactly 48. A 7-face has at most 64 occupied sites by partition into four-cubes and the eight-site four-cube lemma. [Four-cube lemma](PROJECTION_BALL.md#the-middle-arm-must-have-65-through-99-vertices).

## From seven-face counts to the eight-face bound

Let the two outer paths together have m vertices. They have m−2 edges. Sum the component counts of the induced graphs on the sixteen seven-dimensional faces. Each vertex belongs to eight faces and each edge to seven, so the sum is

    8m − 7(m−2) = m+14.

Along a single path, cut its vertex sequence into maximal runs on each side of a coordinate. Each run is one face component because the path is induced. The runs alternate sides, so their counts differ by at most one. Two mutually nonadjacent paths contribute disjoint components, and their combined counts differ by at most two. This also covers a path consisting of a single vertex.

Suppose the two paths use 113 vertices. On every coordinate split, one seven-face contains at least 57 vertices. Its component count is at least nine throughout the possible range of 57 to 64. The opposite seven-face must then contain at least seven components. All sixteen faces together would contribute at least 8×16=128 components, whereas the identity gives 113+14=127. This excludes 113. Any larger pair of paths can be shortened to 113 vertices while keeping both components nonempty. Subtraction from 193 gives the middle bound of 81. The arithmetic verifier also checks every excluded occupancy separately. ## Exhaustion of the small cube geometries

Each split of a base cube into two halves picks a subset on each side. Horizontal edges are determined by those subsets. At a shared address there is one additional vertical edge. Thus a vertex already of degree two in either half cannot occur in the other. After this test, joining two linear forests gives a graph of maximum degree two. If the halves have c and d components and overlap at h addresses, the predicted component count for an acyclic join is c+d−h. A direct traversal checks that count and detects cycles.

The program starts by testing the 65,536 literal four-cube subsets. It builds all required labelled five-cube sets by joining opposite four-cubes. For occupancies 16, 15 and 14 it finds 1,772, 52,832 and 637,440 sets. For the corresponding six-cube occupancies 32, 31 and 30 it finds 4,454, 142,528 and 3,340,064 sets. Coordinate flips and adjacent coordinate swaps generate all hypercube isometries. One half can be normalized by this group, since the other half is tried in every placement.

For a seven-cube with 64−t occupied sites, one six-dimensional half has deficiency at most floor(t/2) from 32. Fix that half up to isometry. For deficiency three it suffices to normalize one of its five-faces and quotient the other by its stabilizer; the resulting 9,968 representatives may repeat some classes. Construct the opposite six-dimensional half from two five-cubes, each constructed from two four-cubes. The join rejects forbidden degree increases and tests the full seven-cube for cycles. This construction lets the opposite half vary over every compatible placement. For occupancies 62 through 64, a separate direct join of the labelled six-cube catalogues gives the same answers. For every reported optimum, the vertex witness is independently verified as an induced linear forest of the stated size and component count. [Enumerator](near_full_faces.cpp), [completed census](near-full-seven-dense.txt).

An additional direct enumeration of every half-full linear forest records all 17,714 labelled eight-cube solutions. Their component counts are 32, 48, 64 or 128. The degree-two filter and a literal traversal suffice for this enumeration, which uses no isometry reduction. [Program](maximum_face_forests.cpp), [counts by dimension](maximum-face-forests.txt).

## A uniform 110-edge bound

Every induced linear forest in the eight-cube has at most **110 edges**, regardless of how many paths it contains. For a forest on m vertices and c components, summing the component counts on all sixteen seven-faces gives m+7c. On opposite faces the two component counts differ by at most c, since each of the c original paths alternates between faces. If their individual lower bounds are a and b, their sum is at least max(a+b, 2 max(a,b)−c). Use the seven-cube census above for populations 57 through 64 and the known seven-cube path maximum of 51 vertices for smaller populations. For each m and c, minimize this expression over the possible population split and multiply by eight. Any result exceeding m+7c excludes that pair. For c=1 we also use the known eight-cube path maximum of 99 vertices. Taking the largest surviving value of m−c over all 128 populations gives 110. Equality, if attainable, requires between 112 and 120 vertices. [Exact inequalities and all occupancies](forest-edge-bound.json), [arithmetic verifier](forest_edge_bound.py), [seven-cube maximum](https://plouffe.fr/OEIS/archive_in_pdf/kochut.pdf), [eight-cube maximum](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00373-014-1423-3).

For a nine-cube snake of L edges, delete the edges of any chosen coordinate. The two remaining face forests have at most 220 edges between them. Therefore every coordinate changes at least L−220 times. In particular, a 233-edge snake would change every coordinate at least 13 times. Its least-used coordinate would change between 13 and 25 times. [The smaller 117-vertex test and cut balance](DENSE_SEVEN_RIGIDITY.md).
