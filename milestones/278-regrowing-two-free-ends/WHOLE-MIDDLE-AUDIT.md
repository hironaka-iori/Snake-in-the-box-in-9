# A fixed middle and two unrestricted outer arms

Fix an ordered induced path $M=(m_0,\dots,m_s)$ in $Q_8$. In $Q_9$, put $M$ in the upper face, retain the two vertical edges at $m_0,m_s$, and allow arbitrary lower-face arms of independent lengths, including zero. A path in this family crosses the distinguished coordinate exactly twice. Reversal, translation and permutation of the eight other axes preserve the problem.

## Necessary conditions for an induced path

1. Every interior upper vertex already has two neighbours on $M$. Its lower copy is forbidden. Both lower ports are mandatory. All other lower vertices are candidates. Each selected lower vertex has a vertical neighbour precisely when it is a port. No further vertices in the upper face occur in a twice-crossing decomposition.
2. The parity of a vertex alternates along the path. At even target length $L=2h$, both endpoints have the majority parity, which occurs $h+1$ times; the opposite parity occurs $h$ times. At odd length $L=2h+1$, each parity occurs $h+1$ times and contributes one endpoint. In the upper face the high bit exchanges even and odd parity. Subtracting the fixed upper-face counts from the whole-path counts gives the exact required lower-face counts. Both endpoints are lower vertices, including a port when its arm has zero edges. At even $L$, both majority cases must be excluded.
3. Write $r=1$ at a port and $r=0$ otherwise. Each selected lower vertex has lower degree exactly $1-r$ when it is an endpoint and exactly $2-r$ otherwise. In the clauses, choosing an endpoint forces its vertex to be present. Every chosen vertex has lower degree between $1-r$ and $2-r$. Selecting its endpoint variable enforces the smaller value; omitting that variable enforces the greater value. Vertices of an ineligible endpoint parity directly receive the latter degree. This handles lower degree zero at a singleton arm. The neighbourhood lists are lists of actual Hamming neighbours, so all edges between selected vertices count.
4. The restriction of any induced path to a coordinate face is a disjoint union of paths, allowing isolated vertices. A square contributes at most three vertices because all four contain the square cycle. A 3-cube contributes at most five. For six chosen vertices, consider the two missing vertices. If they are antipodal, the remaining six form a cycle. Otherwise their distance is one or two, and their closed neighbourhoods intersect. Those neighbourhoods each have size four, so their union omits at least one vertex. That vertex is selected and has all three neighbours selected. A larger selection contains a six-vertex subset and cannot induce a disjoint union of paths either. These arguments also apply when the two arms both enter the same face.
5. Every recorded cycle is independently checked for distinct vertices, physical Hamming adjacency, degree two at each vertex and connectedness. Every upper vertex on the cycle is fixed. Including every lower vertex on the cycle would put a cycle in the induced path. The disjunction excluding at least one such lower vertex is therefore necessary at every target length. A model obeying the local degree conditions can have extra cycles; a constructive answer must pass the full path check.
6. Any longer path in this family can be shortened to $L$ provided $L\ge s+2$. Delete edges successively at the free tips of the lower arms, stopping before either crossing. The path consisting of the middle plus its two ports has $s+2$ edges. Thus exclusions in both parity classes at 192 yield an upper bound of 191 for the entire fixed-middle family, without restrictions on how 192 is divided between the arms.
7. Each cardinality upper bound uses a binary threshold tree. At an internal node assign the variable of rank $j$ true precisely when at least $j$ input literals of its subtree are true. Every merge implication holds because the child subtrees are disjoint. Conversely, induction forces each represented satisfied threshold upward, so forbidding rank $h+1$ enforces an upper bound $h$. Applying this also to complemented input literals gives exact counts. For bounded local degrees, the negative clauses forbid every subset of size one above the maximum and the positive clauses forbid every complement witnessing a shortfall. All conditions in the Boolean formula are necessary.

## Additional four-dimensional face caps

For a four-dimensional coordinate face, its intersection with any full induced path is again an induced linear forest. In faces lying entirely below the middle, the capacity is the maximum size of such a forest among the available vertices. A face which crosses the distinguished coordinate has eight lower positions and eight upper positions. Its available mask is the union of the available lower positions and the fixed occupied upper positions. Every upper position in that mask is mandatory in the candidate geometry, so its count can be subtracted from the maximum forest capacity to give a necessary upper bound on the selected lower count. An unconstrained capacity maximiser may omit mandatory upper positions; this can weaken the cap while preserving necessity. The independent checker assigns every four-cube vertex subset its size when its maximum degree is at most two and peeling all degree-zero and degree-one vertices leaves nothing. Subset maxima then give the capacity for every availability mask. All 196,608 weighted table entries reproduce the Round 277 hash. Both the direct forbidden subsets and the threshold-counter encoding assert the same cap. Each exclusion uses the encoding indicated in its saved row. Donor assignments also check the base and strengthened clauses when the middle was queried with four-cube caps.

## Independent numerical checks

Run `python middle_witnesses.py` beside `whole_middle.py` after the target decisions are saved. It extracts every twice-used axis in the two source archives, transports all ordered vertices of each donor by a recorded cube symmetry and verifies the inverse transport. For each of the 22 middle types it saves a full longest archived donor in both coordinate systems. For each observed length and permissible majority parity it checks an archived donor against all unordered vertex pairs and supplies explicit values for all Boolean variables, including threshold auxiliaries. It evaluates every clause and compares the independently generated formula with the production DIMACS hash. The same independent expansion regenerates the target hashes for all exclusions. The separate LRAT replay against those hashes is recorded by the round audit. The JSON identifies unresolved majority cases separately and reports an exact maximum only when the upper and lower bounds coincide.

The saved Round 188 `lift8to9-fixed95.json` reports UNKNOWN for target 192. Its 96 ordered middle vertices map to the Round 278 representative without reversal. The permutation of old bit positions $0,\dots,7$ is $(0,3,2,1,4,6,7,5)$, followed by translation by 65. This equality identifies the earlier restricted problem as a special case of the two unrestricted arms considered here. The 92- and 93-edge representatives also occur in archived 191-edge paths. The machine-readable file records their individual lower and upper bounds.

The whole-model witness file records the 42 direct parity exclusions. The final [combined coverage](all-middle-port-coverage.json) also checks the exhaustive port partitions for the other two parities and excludes 192 for all 22 middles.
