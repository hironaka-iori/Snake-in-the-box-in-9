## Two crossings and one freely rebuilt arm

Take a coordinate used twice. Removing these two edges leaves two outer paths in one face and an induced middle path $M$ in the other. Project onto the remaining eight coordinates, and call the outer paths $A$ and $B$, oriented outwards from the endpoints of $M$. The inducedness conditions are exact. Each outer arm meets the projection of $M$ only at its own port. The outer arms have disjoint vertex sets and no edge joins them in the eight-cube. In nine dimensions a cross-face chord exists exactly when two extra projected sites coincide. An unwanted edge between outer arms exists exactly when they have sites at Hamming distance one.

If $A$ has $a$ edges, $M$ has $m$, and $B$ has $b$, the total is $a+m+b+2$. When $M$ and $B$ are held fixed, rebuilding $A$ is an ordinary rooted induced-path search. Its forbidden sites are the interior of $M$ and the closed neighbourhood of $B$. A witness at or above a target supplies one at the target by shortening the free tip of $A$, provided the required length of $A$ is nonnegative. [Fixed-short-arm verification](FIXED-SHORT.md)

There is also a bound available before any new search. Write $U_A$ for the maximum one-crossing join at the port of $A$ with $M$ fixed. Deleting $B$ and the crossing to $B$ shows $m+1+a\le U_A$. Hence a two-crossing join of length $L$ requires $b\ge L-U_A-1$. The reversed inequality applies at the other port. The claim concerns the lengths of both outer arms and uses the ceiling for the corresponding directed middle.

## Regrowing the free ends

For a twice-crossed coordinate, remove at most $w$ edges from the free end of each outer arm, always retaining its port. Keep the resulting central path. The search can then regrow both tips in their original eight-dimensional face. Distinct charts can impose the same fixed conditions. Canonicalising the transition words with the middle and the two outer prefixes delimited identifies these cases up to a simultaneous coordinate permutation, translation and reversal of the full path. The original archive, coordinate and map back into the nine-cube remain attached to the representative. [Archive extraction](archive.py)

A stronger search in the full nine-cube applies to any archived path $P=(v_0,\ldots,v_n)$. It fixes only $v_w,\ldots,v_{n-w}$. Any induced path containing these vertices must traverse them consecutively in the given order or its reverse, since each internal vertex has both of its original neighbours. Both new tips can use any of the nine coordinates. The fixed interval and the two free ends place no restriction on the orientation of a cube-symmetric copy.

For either domain, enumerate every extension of the first tip. At each such extension, enumerate all extensions of the second tip, including zero. Each final ordered pair of tails appears exactly once. A new vertex must be unoccupied, adjacent to the active tip and adjacent to no other selected vertex. The first traversal maintains the number of occupied neighbours of each cube vertex. The replay grows the other end first and permanently removes the neighbourhood of a tip as soon as that tip becomes internal. The two calculations compare the number of paths at each length in every case reported as doubly complete. Counts are per labelled, oriented representative. A computation that reaches its node allowance contributes no upper bound. ## Searching directly for 192

For a retained central interval in the full cube, it suffices to decide whether it can occur in a path of exactly 192 edges. Any longer extension can be shortened to 192 by deleting from the two new tails while preserving the interval. The two target-specific traversals use a necessary condition at each current path. The first new vertex at a tip has exactly one occupied neighbour. Every later vertex of that same new tail has zero neighbours in the current path. The part of the new tail after its first vertex therefore lies within a single connected component of the unoccupied, zero-contact sites. This condition still applies if both tips grow. The two tips can enter the same component or distinct components.

Cube parity bounds how many vertices of each colour the tail can take from its component. If both tails enter one component, their combined requirement cannot exceed either colour count. A single path in the component also uses at most two of its vertices of ambient degree zero or one. For two paths the corresponding limit is four. The first traversal uses this extra bound on leaves and degrees. The second traversal reverses the retained interval and explores the other end first. It enumerates the possible extension lengths separately, checks their parity requirements against the available components, and omits the bound on leaves. A closed case requires complete branch coverage; a saved positive path is checked in the original nine-cube. ## Searching without fixed outer arms

The two-crossing model can also retain only $M$ and its crossing ports. Candidate lower-face sites avoid the interior of $M$, and both ports are selected. Each selected vertex has total degree two, apart from two endpoints of total degree one. At each port, one of the incident edges is the prescribed crossing. The parity counts in the nine-cube determine the lower-face parity counts after subtracting $M$. In any square or three-cube of the lower face, respectively at most three or five vertices can be selected, because the induced graph must be a union of paths. The finite capacity check enumerates every subset of these two cubes.

A degree-feasible selection can contain an extra cycle. Its entire vertex set, together with the fixed upper sites on that cycle, cannot belong to an induced path. Forbidding all of its lower vertices from being selected simultaneously is therefore valid. A positive result is assembled as one ordered path in the nine-cube. An exclusion requires a checker-accepted proof for every possible endpoint parity. An unfinished query supplies no such conclusion. ## Consecutive overlap with an archived path

If both complete searches exclude a 192-edge extension of an interval, no 192-edge path contains an isometric copy of that interval. This applies to every occurrence in an archived path, including occurrences outside the original centred position. Every longer extension can be trimmed to 192 while keeping the interval.

For each archive path, list the forbidden intervals by their start and end positions. An interval containing a shorter forbidden interval can be discarded from this list. For each possible start position, the first forbidden right endpoint bounds the length that can still be shared. The calculation verifies every window one edge longer than the reported bound contains a checked forbidden interval. It reconstructs a literal cube isometry for each retained certificate occurrence. The reported upper bound concerns consecutive edges shared in their given order or in reverse. [Interval matching, targeted searches and coverage](overlap.py)

## Comparing two independently grown tails

For an ordered central interval of $c$ edges, grow each possible tail at its own endpoint with the entire interval held fixed. If the two single-ended maxima are $u$ and $v$, a 192-edge join needs $N=192-c$ new edges and is impossible when $u+v<N$. Otherwise retain the left tails of lengths $a$ from $\max(0,N-v)$ through $\min(u,N)$ and right tails of the complementary lengths $N-a$. A compatible pair exists exactly when no two newly added vertices on different tails coincide or have Hamming distance one. All other inducedness conditions already hold in the individual constructions. This criterion also allows an empty tail.

The worker enumerates each end with both an availability mask and an independent occupied-neighbour count. It compares complete depth histograms and, where a pair can still fit, the sorted lists of candidate tails. It checks every required pair both by a closed-neighbourhood mask and by literal Hamming distances. A time, node, memory, tail-storage or pair allowance leaves the case open. A positive path is reconstructed in the original nine-cube and checked at every vertex pair.
