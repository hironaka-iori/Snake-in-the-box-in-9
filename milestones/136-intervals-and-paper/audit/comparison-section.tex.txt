\subsection{Odd-length refinement and surviving cycles}\label{sec:odd-refined-model}
Apply the proved pair floor nine for odd-length snakes and additionally grant
a six-count floor 25 on $0123104$, retaining the inherited unproved rules.
The resulting finite graph has 1601856 states and 4661773 edges. Its
completed-share profile has minimum mean 488, range 585 and comparison
\[
\frac{1103449}{4688}>235,
\]
while the additional-share profile retains mean 496, range 602 and comparison
$551765/2348>234$. Exact integer calculations check the potential on every
retained edge and a cycle attaining each mean. An independent review checks
the saved cycle rows and arithmetic without repeating the full edge scan.
The additional local hypotheses remain unproved, and the complete Lean
integration of the comparison graph remains pending. These are finite-model comparisons~\cite{latercomparisonarchive}.

Both attaining cycles avoid $0123104$. Increasing only its proposed floor
can delete edges or increase weights, preserving the old potential
inequalities. The unchanged cycles still attain the old means. Thus this
single refinement cannot lower either integer comparison. Even zero potential
range gives $68929/293>235$ and $137866/587>234$.

In the first profile, the four-edge cycle has canonical $0120324$ count
vectors, allowing reversal,
\[
(4,5,3,4,4,5),\quad(4,4,3,5,4,4),\quad
(3,4,4,5,3,4),\quad(3,5,4,4,3,5).
\]
Each relevant pair sums to nine. The fourth count is four or five, so
Proposition~\ref{prop:fourth-three-twelve} does not remove these windows.
The other profile has a seven-edge cycle whose shapes avoid the $012032$
pair window. No complete snake realizing either cycle is asserted.

\subsection{Limit of a uniform credit for endpoint occurrences}\label{sec:occurrence-credit-limit}
Theorem~\ref{thm:thirty-two-windows} limits the number of low-pair windows.
The following comparison explains why an occurrence allowance alone cannot
give a smaller length bound in the fixed first profile.

\begin{proposition}[Two-cycle obstruction to uniform credit]\label{prop:uniform-occurrence-credit}
Let $C,R,\mu\ge0$ and $B\ge2$. Suppose
\[
\mu\le488+C/2,\qquad \mu\le3453/7.
\]
Then
\[
\frac{1098960+8\mu+BC+R}{4200+\mu}>235.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $BC\ge2C$, the numerator after subtracting $235(4200+\mu)$ is
at least
\[
(2C-4\mu+1952)+223(3453/7-\mu)+R+37/7>0.
\]
Each parenthesized term is nonnegative, and the denominator is positive.
The strict comparison and its extension to every $B\ge2$ have passed
Lean~\cite{lowpairarchive}.
\end{proof}
The saved four-edge cycle has two relevant suffix occurrences, so credit
$C$ per occurrence bounds its mean by $488+C/2$. A competing seven-edge
cycle has no such occurrence and has mean $3453/7$ under the same
first-profile weights. These cycle data and the comparison formula are
premises of the proposition. The second profile's mean 496 is a different
weighting and must not replace $3453/7$ here. Neither the proved allowance
32 nor the proposed allowance 24 overcomes this obstruction. Further
progress must restrict the competing cycle, change the credit or improve
the global comparison.
