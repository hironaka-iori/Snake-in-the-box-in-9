\begin{theorem}[Endpoint geometry at equality]\label{thm:pair-eight-endpoints}\label{prop:pair-equality-occurrences}
If a fitting canonical $012032$ window satisfies
$\alpha_{r+2}+\alpha_{r+4}=8$, both counted vertices are at Hamming distance
two from both endpoints. At most thirteen such window starts occur in any snake.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Computer-assisted proof and counting]
The pair minima and reversal reduce equality to the caps $(2,6)$, $(3,5)$
and $(4,4)$. For each cap, a complete checked contradiction excludes an
endpoint outside the fourteen-vertex common distance-two sphere in normalized
coordinates. The actual-snake interpretations, all three contradiction
certificates and coordinate transfers are complete~\cite{endpointcompletionarchive}.

The distance-two spheres about two distinct vertices of $\Q_9$ intersect
in at most fourteen vertices. Translation reduces this finite fact to
511 nonzero endpoint differences and the 36 vertices at distance two from
zero. The finite verification and its application to actual path positions
have passed Lean. For a nonempty set $I$ of qualifying starts, the positions
$r+2$, for $r\in I$, are distinct. The further position $\max I+4$ exceeds
all of them and is counted by one of these windows. All $|I|+1$ vertices
lie in the same intersection, so $|I|+1\le14$. The empty case is immediate.
The theorem has no remaining local-certificate premise; attainment of thirteen
is not asserted.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Parity at equality]\label{cor:pair-nine-parity}
Equality of the pair sum to eight requires both $m$ and $r$ to be even.
Consequently $\alpha_{r+2}+\alpha_{r+4}\ge9$ whenever $m$ or $r$ is odd.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Hamming distance two preserves the parity of the number of nonzero cube
coordinates. The preceding theorem gives the same parity to $x_0$, $x_m$
and $x_{r+2}$. Each path edge reverses that parity, so $m$ and $r+2$ are
even. Integrality and the pair floor eight give the result. These parity
deductions have passed Lean~\cite{endpointcompletionarchive}.
\end{proof}

The bound nine for odd-length snakes is sharp. A 149-edge snake has internal
counts $(8,6,10,3,8)$ in the normalized window; a 133-edge snake has counts
$(11,4,13,5,12)$. Their relevant pairs are $(6,3)$ and $(4,5)$. Thus a
uniform pair floor ten fails even when both counts are at least four.
The complete paths, counts and endpoint distances have passed Lean.
Appendix~\ref{app:odd-pair-witnesses} gives their transition sequences.
These examples supply no record construction or realization of an entire
critical comparison-graph cycle~\cite{oddwitnessarchive}.

\subsection{An endpoint forced by a pair sum at most nine}\label{sec:small-pair-endpoint}
\begin{theorem}[Endpoint and common neighbour]\label{thm:small-pair-endpoint}
Suppose a fitting window beginning at $x_r$ has canonical directions
$012032$ and $\alpha_{r+2}+\alpha_{r+4}\le9$. There is an endpoint
$x_e$, with $e\in\{0,m\}$, such that
\[
\Ham(x_e,x_{r+2})=\Ham(x_e,x_{r+4})=2.
\]
Moreover, an unselected vertex $z$ is adjacent to $x_e$ and to each of
$x_r,x_{r+2},x_{r+4},x_{r+6}$. No parity assumption on $m$ or $r$ is needed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Computer-assisted proof and geometric transfer]
Normalize the window to $(0,1,3,7,6,14,10)$. The counted centres are 3 and 6.
Their eligible square lists contain 42 opposite-corner occurrences over
37 distinct vertices; the five shared corners are each counted twice.
Suppose the pair sum is at most nine and neither endpoint has the two
required distances. Give each represented vertex its actual path and
endpoint membership, and interpret each count register by its prefix threshold.
The following necessary conditions hold:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{l r}
\toprule Condition & Selected clauses\\\midrule
The seven window vertices are selected & 7\\
A selected vertex has at most two selected neighbours & 192\\
A selected degree-one vertex is an endpoint & 32\\
Ten specified six-vertex cycles are not wholly selected & 10\\
The 42 corner occurrences have sum at most nine & 435\\
Seven specified vertices are not endpoints & 7\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The last seven vertices are $0,10,18,34,66,130,258$, each at distance two
from both centres. A complete certificate contradicts these 683 clauses.
All clause interpretations, the 13299 deductions and the coordinate transfer
have passed Lean. The selected proof uses neither an endpoint-parity premise
nor a global endpoint-count register~\cite{lowpairarchive}.

The other seven vertices in the common distance-two sphere are
$5,15,23,39,71,135,263$. They are adjacent to the selected internal vertex 7,
whose two path neighbours exhaust its selected degree, so none is selected.
A qualifying endpoint therefore belongs to the displayed seven-vertex list.
Every member of that list and the alternate window vertices $0,3,6,10$ is
adjacent to vertex 2. Transfer this neighbour to the original coordinates.
It is absent: a selected vertex adjacent to both $x_r$ and $x_{r+6}$ would
have to be consecutive to both, which is impossible. The stronger
common-neighbour conclusion is also checked~\cite{occurrencearchive}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:pair-ten-without-endpoint}
If neither endpoint is at distance two from both counted centres of a
fitting $012032$ window, then $\alpha_{r+2}+\alpha_{r+4}\ge10$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use the contrapositive of Theorem~\ref{thm:small-pair-endpoint} and integrality.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Counting windows assigned to an endpoint}\label{sec:endpoint-occurrences}
\begin{lemma}[Count for one endpoint]\label{lem:sixteen-endpoint-windows}
Fix an endpoint and a set of fitting six-transition window starts. Suppose
each window's four alternate vertices and that endpoint share an unselected
neighbour. Then there are at most sixteen starts.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Orient the path from the endpoint and translate it to zero, using the same
transformed path for all windows. Write its first successor as $\{b\}$,
identifying a cube vertex with its nonzero coordinates. A window's common
neighbour with the endpoint is a unit vertex $\{a\}$, with $a\ne b$
because it is unselected. Its selected neighbours have even parity. Every
window start is therefore even, and dividing the starts by two preserves
their distinctness.

Let $I$ be these sampled starts. Each $r\in I$ covers $[r,r+3]$ and is
assigned its common coordinate. Let $A$ be the coordinates used and $a=|A|$.
They exclude $b$, so $a\le8$. If $u<v$ have different common coordinates
and $v<u+3$, their windows share two positive sampled positions. The only
common neighbours of two distinct unit vertices are zero and their pair
vertex. Both shared positions would equal that pair, violating injectivity.
Thus differently labelled starts satisfy $u+3\le v$.

For nonempty $I$, this separation implies
\[
|I|+2a+1\le\left|\bigcup_{r\in I}[r,r+3]\right|.
\]
Insert the starts in increasing order. The first contributes four positions.
Each later start contributes at least one new position. If its coordinate
has not occurred previously, separation supplies three new positions,
accounting for the additional two in $2a$.

A selected pair containing $b$ is adjacent to the first successor and hence
can occur only at path index two. Every covered position other than sampled
indices zero and one is consequently a pair among the other eight coordinates,
incident to $A$. There are $7a-\binom a2$ such pairs. Distinct positions
represent distinct vertices, so
\[
\left|\bigcup_{r\in I}[r,r+3]\right|\le7a-\binom a2+2.
\]
Hence $|I|\le1+5a-\binom a2\le16$; the last inequality follows from
$(a-5)(a-6)\ge0$ for integral $a$. The empty case is immediate.
Reversal sends a fitting start $r$ to $m-r-6$ injectively, covering either
endpoint. The normalization, parity, separation, capacity and full count
have passed Lean~\cite{occurrencearchive}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Number of low-pair windows]\label{thm:thirty-two-windows}
Every snake in $\Q_9$ has at most thirty-two fitting canonical $012032$
windows with $\alpha_{r+2}+\alpha_{r+4}\le9$. Windows may overlap, and
each starting position is counted once.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assign a window to the initial endpoint whenever it admits the unselected
common neighbour in Theorem~\ref{thm:small-pair-endpoint}, and assign every
remaining window to the final endpoint. The two groups are disjoint and
exhaustive. Lemma~\ref{lem:sixteen-endpoint-windows} bounds each by sixteen.
The resulting actual-snake theorem has passed Lean~\cite{occurrencearchive}.
\end{proof}
The sixteen-window statement retains its common-neighbour hypothesis. The
two distance equalities alone are a broader assignment condition. The
thirteen-window theorem uses both endpoints and specifically pair sum eight;
it cannot be applied separately to the groups above.

\subsection{Missing pairs and the proposed stronger count}\label{sec:missing-pair-occurrences}
\begin{lemma}[Missing opposite pair]\label{lem:missing-opposite-pair}
Normalize an endpoint to zero. If $j\ge2$ and consecutive sampled vertices
are $x_j=\{a,b\}$ and $x_{j+2}=\{a,c\}$ for distinct coordinates, then
$x_{j+1}=\{a,b,c\}$ and $\{b,c\}$ is absent from the entire snake.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The possible intermediaries are $\{a\}$ and $\{a,b,c\}$. The former would
be adjacent to the initial endpoint at a nonconsecutive position. The triple
is forced. If the opposite pair were selected, the triple would have a third
selected neighbour. Both deductions have passed Lean~\cite{occurrencearchive}.
\end{proof}

Within a consecutive interval sharing one coordinate, the other coordinates
are distinct. Each successive pair of other coordinates is missing by the
lemma. For a set $A$ of common coordinates, such a list has length at most
the maximum of one and the number of available coordinates outside $A$, plus
the number of eligible missing pairs incident to $A$. If an outside coordinate
occurs, count each consecutive block in $A$ against distinct incident edges
of the list. Otherwise the vertex count exceeds the edge count by one.
The list inequality and its application to actual snake vertices are checked.

Let $F$ be the missing pairs meeting $A$, put $t=|F|$, and, for $u\in A$,
let $d_u$ count its incident missing pairs and $s_u$ those going outside $A$.
The identities used to sum interval estimates are
\[
|\{e\in F:u\notin e\}|=t-d_u,\qquad
\sum_{u\in A}d_u\ge t,\qquad
\sum_{u\in A}(d_u+s_u)=2t.
\]
An internal pair contributes two to the last sum; a crossing pair contributes
one degree and one crossing degree. If $t=3$, at most one coordinate has
$s_u=3$, since each crossing pair has only one member in $A$. These
finite-set statements have passed Lean~\cite{occurrencearchive}.

A written interval argument proposes twelve assigned windows per endpoint,
or eleven when no counted window has that endpoint as a boundary. Its complete
assembly is still under verification. Twenty-four remains a proposed total;
Theorem~\ref{thm:thirty-two-windows} is the completed count used here.
Neither count alone improves the upper bound on snake length.
Section~\ref{sec:occurrence-credit-limit} gives a separate obstruction to the
current uniform-credit comparison.
