\begin{theorem}[Components of the missing-pair graph]
\label{thm:missing-pair-components}
Let $q$ have no selected neighbour in a nontrivial snake in $\Q_9$.
Translate $q$ to zero, and join two coordinate directions in a graph $H$
exactly when their weight-two vertex is unselected. Then $H$ is connected
or is the disjoint union of exactly two complete graphs.
In the latter case, if their sizes are $a,b$, then
\[
a+b=9,\qquad m=2ab-2\in\{14,26,34,38\}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{d(q)=0\ \Longrightarrow\ e(q)\ge8,}
\qquad
\boxed{\sum_q r_{12}(q)\le95\ \Longrightarrow\ d(q)\ge1\text{ for every }q.}
\]
For a snake longer than 38 edges, $H$ is connected at every undominated centre.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Zero is unselected, since every selected vertex has a selected neighbour.
All unit vertices are unselected by the hypothesis on $q$. Thus edges of
$H$ correspond exactly to empty squares through zero.

Suppose $H$ is disconnected and choose two components $A,B$.
Every pair $\{a,b\}$ with $a\in A$, $b\in B$ is selected. Consider the
set consisting of these cross-pairs and the candidate triples formed
from an edge of $H$ in one component and a coordinate in the other.
Only its intersection with the selected path is considered below;
candidate triples need not all be selected.

A selected neighbour of a cross-pair is a triple $\{a,b,k\}$ with three
distinct coordinates. If $k$ belongs to neither component, all three
pair sides are selected, contradicting maximum degree two. If $k\in A$
but $ak$ is not an edge of $H$, the same contradiction applies; likewise
for $B$. Thus every selected neighbour is a candidate triple in the set.
Conversely, a selected candidate triple already has two distinct selected
cross-pair neighbours. Its remaining pair side is absent, and degree two
excludes every selected weight-four neighbour. The selected portion of
the set is therefore nonempty and closed under selected adjacency.

A nonempty subset of a path closed under adjacency contains the whole
path: start at any vertex in the subset and follow the path in both
directions. Therefore every selected vertex belongs to this set.
A third component would supply a selected pair meeting $A$ and that
component, outside the set. A nonedge within $A$ or within $B$ would also
supply a selected pair outside it. Both are impossible. Hence there are
exactly two components, and each is complete.

There are now exactly $ab$ selected pair vertices, and all other selected
vertices are triples of degree two. Both endpoints are pair vertices.
The path alternates between pairs and triples, so it contains $ab-1$
triples and has $2ab-2$ edges. The four possible unordered component sizes
are $1+8$, $2+7$, $3+6$, and $4+5$, giving the stated lengths.

If $H$ is connected, it has at least eight edges. In the disconnected
case its edge count is $\binom a2+\binom b2$, respectively 28, 22, 18,
or 16 for those four sizes. In every case $e(q)=|E(H)|\ge8$.
An undominated centre therefore contributes at least $12\cdot8=96$ to
the nonnegative loss sum, proving the final implication.
\end{proof}

All four disconnected-case lengths are attained. List the $a\times b$
cross-pairs row by row, reversing the order in alternate rows, and insert
the union triple between each consecutive pair. Each triple has exactly
its two intended selected pair neighbours, so this is an induced path.
Direct checks of distinctness and every pairwise adjacency confirm the
four constructions. This does not establish attainability of the lower
bound $e(q)=8$ in the connected case.

The component theorem is a written geometric proof with an independent
review. Lean has checked the required path degree and endpoint lemmas,
the coordinate pair-and-triple implications, and the statement that a
nonempty selected subset closed under adjacency contains the whole path.
The actual candidate subset, its closure, component classification,
and final graph and loss counting still require assembly in Lean.
No improved unconditional numerical length bound is claimed.
