\subsection{Actual state sequences and exclusion filters}\label{sec:state-filter-bridge}
The finite comparison requires a consistent sequence of states for every
snake, followed by a justification of each rejection test. These are separate
from checking the numerical potential. Write $d_i$ for the direction from
$x_i$ to $x_{i+1}$, and let $\operatorname{can}$ relabel coordinates in order of
first appearance. Thus equal canonical words specify the same equality
relations among directions. For a word $w$, write $w[a,b)$ for its entries
from $a$ through $b-1$.

Let $P=(x_0,\ldots,x_m)$ with $m\ge9$. Define
\[
u_r=\operatorname{can}(d_r\cdots d_{r+7}),\qquad
a_r=\operatorname{can}(d_r\cdots d_{r+8}).
\]
Here $u_r$ is defined for $0\le r\le m-8$, and $a_r$ for $0\le r<m-8$.
The symbol $a_r$ is a canonical nine-direction word, rather than a cube
coordinate. Relabel each identifier in the dictionary of
Section~\ref{sec:exact-history} by its unique canonical word in the ordered
505-entry table. This bijection preserves suffixes and their lengths.
Let $h_r$ be the longest listed suffix of $a_0\cdots a_{r-1}$ in this
relabelled dictionary, and define
\[
c_r=\sum_{j=0}^{4}(b_{r+3+j}-1)6^{4-j},\qquad
\widehat c_r=\sum_{j=0}^{5}(b_{r+3+j}-1)6^{5-j}.
\]
The second expression is used only at a fitting nine-direction window.
Subtraction by one makes the symbols $1,\ldots,6$ into base-six digits.

\begin{proposition}[Consistent actual states]\label{prop:actual-state-sequence}
Every snake with $m\ge9$ determines $m-7$ states, indexed by
$0\le r\le m-8$, and $m-8$ consecutive candidate transitions. Its state at
$r$ records $(u_r,h_r,c_r)$. At transition $r<m-8$, the source and target
count codes are respectively
\[
\left\lfloor\frac{\widehat c_r}{6}\right\rfloor=c_r,
\qquad \widehat c_r\bmod6^5=c_{r+1},
\]
and the entering count is $\widehat c_r\bmod6+1=b_{r+8}$.
The history update gives $h_{r+1}$ and satisfies both direction-shape
compatibility tests of the model. These assertions do not yet assert that
the transition occurs in the saved retained-edge array.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every consecutive segment of an induced path is induced. Canonicalization
therefore places each eight-direction window among the 132 recorded shapes
and each nine-direction window among the 505 recorded extensions. The finite
coverage checks enumerate every canonical induced word of the relevant
length; they do not assume that a selected sample exhausts actual windows.
Canonicalizing the first or last eight entries of $a_r$ gives $u_r$ or
$u_{r+1}$, respectively, because equality of coordinates is preserved under
restriction and relabelling.

All represented counts are internal, so $1\le b_i\le6$. The displayed
quotient and remainder identities follow by separating the first and last
base-six digits. Lemma~\ref{lem:longest-suffix} gives the exact history
update. A nonempty history ends in the preceding nine-direction symbol;
its compatible shape is consequently the target shape of that symbol.
The empty history leaves the shape explicit. Thus the dictionary gives
$132+74$ shape/history possibilities, each with $6^5$ count codes, and all
actual states lie in the stated set of $1601856$ states. Consecutive
transitions have the same intermediate state, including the terminal state
at $r=m-8$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Interval and short-rule retention]\label{prop:local-filter-retention}
Every actual candidate transition passes the ten completed four-centre
interval exclusions and the forty completed short-count exclusions of the
current model, in both orientations and at every applicable offset.
An offset is applicable only when the whole direction window fits and all
tested counts occur among $b_{r+3},\ldots,b_{r+8}$. The sum tests require
each participating symbol to be less than six.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
An interval entry specifies four consecutive internal counts and the five
directions through them. Its three possible offsets are $s=0,1,2$, with
directions beginning at $r+s+2$ and counts beginning at $r+s+3$. The decoded
digits are exactly the corresponding truncated singleton counts. Each
interval $[\ell,h]$ in these entries has $\ell\le6$ and either $h<6$ or
$h=21$. For an internal count $\alpha\le21$,
\[
\ell\le\min\{6,\alpha\}\le h
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\ell\le\alpha\le h.
\]
Thus a matching encoded interval would satisfy the actual-count hypotheses
of its completed local refutation, a contradiction.

The forty short rules comprise 37 cap tests and three sum tests. Every cap
is strictly below six, so a passing cap on $b_i$ is the same cap on
$\alpha_i$. In a sum test, the explicit conditions $b_i<6$ give
$b_i=\alpha_i$ at every tested position. The encoded forbidden sum would
therefore contradict its established actual-count inequality. Reversing a
window of $L$ directions replaces its internal position $j$ by $L-j$;
reversing the snake preserves the singleton counts and all induced-path
conditions. This proves the same conclusions for the jointly reversed
direction and count tests. The ten refutations and forty local inequalities
are individually checked inputs to this argument; completeness of their
placement and decoding is a separate checked assertion.
\end{proof}

Three of the remembered signatures have a stronger property than general
history recognition. For an eleven-direction word $w$, define its overlap
signature by
\[
\mathcal S(w)=\bigl(\operatorname{can}(w[0,9)),
\operatorname{can}(w[1,10)),\operatorname{can}(w[2,11))\bigr).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Three unambiguous exclusions]\label{prop:history-exclusion-retention}
Suppose transition $r$ emits $\mathcal S(w)$ for one of the following
canonical words. Then $r\ge2$, the canonical eleven-direction window
beginning at $s=r-2$ is exactly $w$, and the indicated count bound holds:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
w&\text{count bound}\\\hline
01230432543&\alpha_{s+10}\ge3\\
01234532013&\alpha_{s+8}\ge3\\
01234532053&\alpha_{s+10}\ge3
\end{array}
\]
Consequently none of the three history exclusions, which require the
corresponding stored symbol to be at most two, rejects an actual snake.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The history invariant identifies an emitted triple with
$(a_{r-2},a_{r-1},a_r)$ and prohibits emission at $r=0,1$. These are precisely
the overlapping nine-direction slices of the actual eleven-direction
window beginning at $s$. Its first nine canonical labels are fixed by the
first signature entry. Every canonical label is below nine, so there are
only $9^2=81$ possibilities for the remaining pair. For each displayed
signature, exact finite checking retains only the displayed word after
requiring canonical order of first appearance, induced-path validity,
and equality of the other two canonical slices. This check covers every
compatible word in nine coordinates.

Theorem~\ref{thm:three-forced-corners} now supplies the stated count bounds.
Since $s=r-2$, their positions are $r+8$, $r+6$, and $r+8$. These are the
sixth, fourth, and sixth stored counts, respectively. Truncating a count
at six preserves the lower bound three, which contradicts every tested
upper bound two.
\end{proof}

For the reference family, let $\mathcal F_{235}$ contain the 1287 keys of
the six-centre catalogue, and let $\mathcal F_{236}$ contain the 732 keys
of the earlier radius-four catalogue. A key is a canonical direction word
together with its internal count symbols. Let $\mathsf C_{235}$ and
$\mathsf C_{236}$ denote, separately, the assertions that every local
necessary-condition system prescribed by the respective catalogue is
refuted. These are the two catalogue premises used below.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional reference retention]\label{prop:reference-union-retention}
Assume $\mathsf C_{235}$ and $\mathsf C_{236}$. Every actual candidate
transition avoids the reference family in both orientations. After joint
reversal and canonical relabelling, this family has
\[
1287+732-641=1378
\]
keys and 2678 distinct oriented direction-and-count pairs. For a key with
$w$ count symbols, where $2\le w\le6$, every offset $s$ with $s+w\le6$
is covered.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The exact union takes all keys of $\mathcal F_{235}$ and 91 additional
keys of $\mathcal F_{236}$. Checked source indices identify every chosen
representative with a key of one original catalogue. Each has $w+1$
directions and $w$ symbols in $\{1,\ldots,6\}$.

At offset $s$, the extracted count code is
\[
\left\lfloor\frac{\widehat c_r}{6^{6-s-w}}\right\rfloor\bmod6^w.
\]
Uniqueness of a fixed-length base-six expansion identifies these digits
with $b_{r+s+3},\ldots,b_{r+s+w+2}$. The matching directions start at
$r+s+2$. A symbol below six gives equality to the actual count; symbol
six gives its full interval $[6,21]$. Translation and coordinate
normalization therefore turn a hypothetical match into a realization
of a refuted local system, contradicting the relevant catalogue premise.
Reversal reverses both lists and preserves this argument. Checking the
union's entries and indices establishes their coverage; it does not
discharge either catalogue-refutation premise.
\end{proof}

These results connect the stated filters to arbitrary snakes. Completing
the charge-model proof still requires identification of each actual step
with its exact retained-array entry and justification of every assigned
credit. A general three-window signature may admit several compatible
eleven-direction words; all must support the assigned bound on
$\sum_{i=4}^{8}\sigma_{12}(x_{r+i})$. Bounds on those same five centres
combine by maximum. The complete potential and its range must then be
checked and summed along this actual walk. The present interface results
alone yield no improvement to the upper bound 235. The source catalogues,
finite comparisons and checked state and filter proofs are preserved in the
interface archive~\cite{interfacearchive}. The union cardinalities are checked
by exact source-table comparison; membership of every chosen key and the
conditional retention argument have Lean proofs. Historical closed catalogue
proofs and their saved audits are available, while their complete compatible
dependencies have not been imported into the current checking environment.
