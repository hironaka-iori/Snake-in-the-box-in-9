\subsection{When an added charge can improve the cycle mean}\label{sec:bonus-criterion}
The next definition separates the size of a proposed charge from the cycles
on which it contributes. All graphs in this subsection are finite and have
at least one directed cycle; all weights are rational.

\begin{definition}[Critical cycles and the zero-bonus subgraph]\label{def:zero-bonus}
For a weighted directed graph $(G,w)$, let $\mathcal C(G)$ be its finite set
of simple directed cycles and define
\[
\overline w(C)=\frac{1}{|C|}\sum_{e\in C}w(e),\qquad
\mu(G,w)=\min_{C\in\mathcal C(G)}\overline w(C).
\]
A cycle $C$ is critical if $\overline w(C)=\mu(G,w)$. For a nonnegative
edge bonus $B$, define the zero-bonus subgraph
\[
G_B^0=\bigl(V(G),\{e\in E(G):B(e)=0\}\bigr).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Exact criterion for improving the cycle mean]\label{prop:bonus-criterion}
Keep $G$ fixed and let $B(e)\ge0$ for every edge. Then
\[
\mu(G,w+B)\ge\mu(G,w),
\]
with equality if and only if $G_B^0$ contains a critical cycle of $(G,w)$.
Equivalently, the minimum mean increases strictly if and only if every
previously critical cycle has positive total bonus.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For every simple cycle,
\[
\overline{w+B}(C)=\overline w(C)+\frac{1}{|C|}\sum_{e\in C}B(e)
\ge\overline w(C)\ge\mu(G,w).
\]
Taking the minimum proves monotonicity. If a critical cycle lies in $G_B^0$,
its weight is unchanged and still attains the original minimum, proving
equality.

Conversely, suppose the minima are equal. The nonempty finite set of simple
cycles contains a cycle attaining the new minimum. Both inequalities above
must then be equalities for that cycle. It was critical before the bonus,
and its total bonus is zero. Every summand is nonnegative, so every edge of
the cycle has bonus zero, placing the cycle in $G_B^0$. This proves the
equivalence. Since the new minimum cannot decrease, strict increase is
equivalent to the absence of such a cycle, or to positive total bonus on
every old critical cycle. Finiteness guarantees the attainment used here.
The minimum-attainment, equality, strictness and nonnegative-sum implications
have been checked in Lean for rational cycle means.
\end{proof}

This criterion gives an exact test for a proposed family of additional
shares on a fixed finite graph: their combined positive increments must
meet every critical cycle. Increasing a single witnessed cycle is insufficient
when another critical cycle receives no bonus. The condition determines
whether the finite mean increases; it does not by itself guarantee the
strict numerical margin in~\eqref{eq:strict234}, bound the potential range,
or prove that the additional shares are valid for actual snakes. When several
share bounds concern the same five centres, first combine them by their
maximum and define $B$ as the resulting change in edge weight. This preserves
the accounting rule for overlapping alternatives.
