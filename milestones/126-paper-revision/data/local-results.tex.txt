\subsection{A restriction on four outer counts}\label{sec:outer-012031}
\begin{proposition}[An outer count at least four]\label{prop:outer-012031}
For every fitting six-transition segment of pattern $012031$,
\[
\max\{\alpha_{r+1},\alpha_{r+2},\alpha_{r+4},\alpha_{r+5}\}\ge4.
\]
The middle count has no additional restriction, and the conclusion also
holds after reversal.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Computer-assisted proof]
Normalize the segment and suppose all four indicated counts are at most
three. The necessary selected-vertex, degree and missing-corner conditions
have a checked contradiction with 5956 deductions. Its complete count
interpretation, replay and transfer to arbitrary snakes have passed Lean.
The reversed canonical word represents the same pattern and exchanges the
four outer positions. Three further reference entries follow from this
restriction. Sharpness for complete snakes remains unresolved
~\cite{latersumarchive}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A sharp pair bound for the pattern $012032$}\label{sec:pair-012032}
\begin{theorem}[Second and fourth counts on $012032$]\label{thm:pair-012032}
Suppose $r+6\le m$ and the six transitions beginning at $x_r$ have canonical
pattern $012032$. Then
\[
\alpha_{r+2}\ge2,\qquad \alpha_{r+4}\ge2,\qquad
\alpha_{r+2}+\alpha_{r+4}\ge8.
\]
The sum bound is sharp for complete snakes. The pairs $(2,6)$, $(3,5)$ and
$(4,4)$ all occur; reversal also gives $(6,2)$ and $(5,3)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Computer-assisted proof]
Normalize the selected segment to $(0,1,3,7,6,14,10)$. Complete checked
contradictions exclude a second count at most one, a pair bounded by $(2,5)$,
and a pair bounded by $(3,4)$. The necessary clauses use actual selected
vertices, their path degrees and the missing-corner interpretation of each
count. Their interpretations, complete contradiction replays and coordinate
transport have passed Lean. Reversal exchanges the second and fourth
positions and returns the same canonical direction pattern.

Thus both counts are at least two. If their sum were at most seven, arrange
the smaller first by reversal. It is either two, with the other at most five,
or three, with the other at most four. Both cases are excluded. The complete
142-, 138- and 144-edge snakes in Appendix~\ref{app:pair-012032-witnesses}
attain the three displayed pairs. Their induced-path conditions, designated
directions and singleton counts are checked in Lean~\cite{pair012032archive}.
\end{proof}

For this pattern, the case $(2,6)$ also forces both endpoints to lie at
Hamming distance two from both counted vertices; its actual-snake proof
is complete. Reversal gives the corresponding $(6,2)$ conclusion. The
analogous statement for every pair with sum eight remains conditional:
the remaining boundary cases require their complete checked deductions.
The three printed witnesses satisfy the stronger endpoint statement,
which alone does not prove it for every snake~\cite{endpointequalityarchive}.

\begin{proposition}[A conditional limit on equality occurrences]\label{prop:pair-equality-occurrences}
Assume every $012032$ window with second and fourth counts summing to eight
has both counted vertices at distance two from both endpoints. Then at most
13 such windows occur in a snake.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The distance-two spheres about distinct vertices of $\Q_9$ intersect in at
most 14 vertices. Translation reduces this finite fact to 511 nonzero
endpoint differences and the 36 vertices at distance two from zero; the
finite verification and its application to actual path positions have
passed Lean. For a nonempty set $I$ of qualifying starts, the first counted
positions $r+2$, for $r\in I$, are distinct. The further position
$\max I+4$ exceeds all of them and is also counted by one of these windows.
All $|I|+1$ vertices lie in the same intersection, so $|I|+1\le14$.
The empty case is immediate~\cite{endpointequalityarchive}.
\end{proof}
This proposition retains the endpoint hypothesis. It supplies no bound on
the number of six-count groups with sum below 25.

\subsection{Necessary counts in an unresolved seven-transition window}\label{sec:critical-six}
\begin{proposition}[Low-total $0120324$ windows]\label{prop:critical-six-intervals}
Suppose $r+7\le m$, the seven-transition pattern is $0120324$, and
$\sum_{j=1}^{6}\alpha_{r+j}\le22$. Then the six counts lie respectively in
\[
([1,21],[2,21],[1,21],[3,21],[1,21],[1,21]).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The prefix has pattern $012032$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:pair-012032} gives
$\alpha_{r+2},\alpha_{r+4}\ge2$. If $\alpha_{r+4}=2$, a checked prefix
exclusion for this shape rules out $\alpha_{r+1}\le2$ when
$\alpha_{r+4}\le3$, and hence gives $\alpha_{r+1}\ge3$. The suffix beginning
at $r+1$ has canonical pattern $012314$, with middle count $\alpha_{r+4}$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:outer-four-sum} therefore gives
\[
\alpha_{r+2}+\alpha_{r+3}+\alpha_{r+5}+\alpha_{r+6}\ge19.
\]
The six-count sum would be at least $3+2+19=24$, a contradiction.
Consequently $\alpha_{r+4}\ge3$. Positivity and the internal upper bound
21 give all other intervals. The prefix exclusion, canonical suffix
identification and complete deduction for arbitrary snakes have passed
Lean~\cite{criticalsixarchive}.
\end{proof}
This necessary condition leaves the proposed floor 23 unresolved. The
selected older pair and five-count inequalities, together with the reviewed
triple exclusion, permit the numerical tuple $(2,4,3,4,2,3)$ of total 18.
No snake realizing it is asserted. It shows that this selected family of
inequalities alone cannot prove 23; the review does not exhaust all earlier
catalogue entries.

Three full-cube searches for totals at most 24, at most 22, and at most 22
with the proved intervals all reached their time limits. Their 126 corner
occurrences retain repetitions over 100 distinct corners. The 35 added
cycle exclusions and the encoded conditions were independently checked,
but no search produced a complete model or contradiction. These outcomes
establish no additional count floor~\cite{criticalsixarchive}.
