# Round 242. Direct 192-edge construction from the closest eight-count changes

The target is a 193-vertex induced path in \(Q_9\). We checked every placement of the Round 241 signed change against the 131 saved 191-edge seeds. The smallest number of conflicting memberships is six. We then constructed 56,406 distinct proposed minority classes from the closest placements and left the opposite class and path order free. Every proposed class fails a necessary Hamiltonian path condition.

## Coverage and construction

The [previous equality classification](../241-exact-count-distance/EQUALITY_CASES.md) gives exactly two orientations per support. Choose the four-block, then the singleton, then an unordered partition of the remaining four coordinates into two pairs. This gives \(\binom94\cdot5\cdot3=1890\) coordinate partitions. Within a fixed partition the eight affected positions are a translate of the subgroup generated by the flips of the four-block and the two full pairs. Fixing one coordinate to zero in each of these three blocks chooses exactly one translation per coset. Of the 64 representatives, 32 give each affected parity. Translating within a coset preserves or reverses the character, so taking both signs is sufficient. Conversely the support reveals the singleton as its constant coordinate, and the three other blocks by which coordinate pairs have constant XOR throughout. Thus there are \(1890\cdot32\cdot2=120960\) signed placements per affected parity.

The [orbit calculation](seed_orbit.py) first verifies all 131 ordered paths for labels, distinctness and adjacency at all pairs. They give 78 distinct even and 67 distinct odd 96-vertex sets. For the deletion set \(D\), insertion set \(B\) and a seed class \(U\), the number of conflicts is \(|D\setminus U|+|B\cap U|\). Zero is necessary and sufficient to execute the signed change as given. The [complete histogram and all per-class closest placements](seed-orbit.json) cover 17,539,200 comparisons. Exactly 28 comparisons attain six conflicts, across 17 classes. Fourteen have five missing deletions and one occupied insertion, and fourteen have four missing deletions and two occupied insertions.

For each of these 28 placements, remove the available deletions and keep as many available insertions as the deletion count allows, in all possible ways. The first case has 15 deletions and 19 available insertions; the second has 16 and 18. This gives
\[
14\binom{19}{15}+14\binom{18}{16}=56406
\]
distinct proposed 96-vertex minority classes. For each class, join two of its vertices if some opposite vertex has precisely those two neighbours in the class. A 192-edge snake would traverse a Hamiltonian path of this graph; its two ends must each have a possible majority endpoint with exactly that one neighbour. This is the fixed-minority criterion from [Round 232](../232-minority-contraction/MATHEMATICAL_NOTE.md). A total of 56,168 classes have an isolated vertex. The remaining 238 have more than two graph leaves or a leaf ineligible as an end. These are direct finite graph checks. The solver branch was never needed. ## A lower bound of 21 changed counts relative to each saved seed

Let \(f\) be the ternary indicator difference on one parity side and \(w=Af\) its nine-neighbour sums. Write \(v(p)\in\{-1,1\}^9\) for the sign coordinates of a profile position. The weight-two character identity gives
\[
\sum_p w(p)v(p)v(p)^{\mathsf T}=(\sum_p w(p))I_9\pmod5.
\]
Each weight-one character sum of \(w\) is divisible by seven. We use the sign-cube moment and support lemmas proved in [Round 241](../241-exact-count-distance/MATHEMATICAL_NOTE.md).

**Balanced side.** Suppose \(\sum f=0\) and \(w\) has nine nonzero positions. The positions with nonzero residues modulo five cannot number from one through seven by the degree-two moment lemma. If there are none, all nine amplitudes are \(\pm5\), which cannot sum to zero. If all nine survive, their sign rows span a totally isotropic subspace of a nondegenerate nine-dimensional form over \(\mathbb F_5\), of dimension at most four. After normalizing by a basis row, at most eight distinct projective sign columns are possible. The nine vertices lie on the same parity side and hence give distinct projective columns. This is a contradiction.

There are therefore eight surviving residues and a ninth position \(z\) of amplitude \(\pm5\). The four-block reduction in Round 241 depends only on those eight residues and the zero moment matrix. Translate and permute the cube so that sign coordinates within each of four blocks agree at all eight positions. For two coordinates in one block, subtract their weight-one sums modulo seven. Only \(w(z)(v_i(z)-v_j(z))\) remains. Its vanishing forces \(v_i(z)=v_j(z)\). Thus \(z\) is constant on all four blocks, yet all eight such words of its parity are already occupied. Balanced support nine is impossible. The saved fixed-minority exclusions require nonzero change, and the present scan excludes eight. The balanced side consequently changes at least ten counts.

**Growing side.** Here \(\sum f=1,\ \sum w=9\). Round 241 shows that nine changed counts force a single addition, already excluded by the finite majority-class covers for the saved bank. We prove that exactly ten positions are impossible for any such ternary change. The moment matrix has rank nine modulo five, so at least nine of the ten residues survive.

If exactly nine survive, the square-matrix argument of Round 241 gives nine pairwise distance-two positions forming \(N(q)\). In detail, for the sign columns \(X\) and diagonal weights \(D\), the identity \(XDX^{\mathsf T}=9I\) implies \(X^{\mathsf T}X=9D^{-1}\). Its diagonal forces weights one modulo five, and its off-diagonal forces distance two. The remaining position is \(t\). Put \(h=f-\mathbf1_{\{q\}}\), so \(Ah=0\bmod5\). As \(f=Aw\bmod2\) and \(f\) is ternary, \(h\) is supported in \(N(N(q))\cup N(t)\), of size at most \(1+36+9=46\). If \(h\) is nonzero modulo five, its antipodally even extension \(h(x)+h(\bar x)\) has degree two: the only singular character weights of \(A\) modulo five are two and seven, and symmetrization removes seven. This nonzero quadratic needs at least 128 vertices, or 64 on the original parity side. Thus \(h=0\bmod5\), which forces \(h=0\) since its integer values lie in \([-2,1]\). The resulting profile has only nine positions.

If all ten residues survive, \(X\) has ten columns. The symmetric matrix \(B=9D^{-1}-X^{\mathsf T}X\) has rank one because \(DX^{\mathsf T}X/9\) is a rank-nine projection. Write \(B=a h h^{\mathsf T}\) over \(\mathbb F_5\) with \(a\ne0\). The relation \(BDX^{\mathsf T}=0\) shows \(h\) has at least two nonzero entries. Distinct positions have zero sign dot product precisely at distance two: the dot product is \(9-2d\), and \(d\in\{2,4,6,8\}\). Every zero coordinate of \(h\) therefore indexes a point at distance two from all others, and points indexed by nonzero coordinates are pairwise at distance greater than two.

If \(h\) has a zero coordinate, translate that position to zero. The nonzero-coordinate group now consists of disjoint two-subsets, at most four. The other group has at least six points at pairwise distance two. They lie in the neighbourhood of one vertex: translate one to zero and use that a pairwise-intersecting family of at least four distinct two-subsets has a common coordinate. Any further vertex at distance two from all six also belongs to that neighbourhood. After moving the centre to zero, a weight-three vertex can be at distance two from only three unit vectors; all other possible vertices have weight one. This would place ten distinct vertices in a nine-element neighbourhood. If \(h\) has no zero coordinate, the ten neighbourhoods are disjoint. At a point with even amplitude, \(f=Aw\bmod2\) would force all nine neighbouring \(f\) values to vanish by ternarity. Its amplitude would be zero. All ten amplitudes must therefore be odd, contrary to their sum of nine. This proves the exclusion of ten.

Thus for each saved seed the growing side changes at least eleven opposite-side counts and the balanced side changes at least ten. The profile supports are disjoint. **Any 192-edge snake must differ from each saved 191-edge seed in at least 21 labelled counts.** This conclusion combines written proofs with native finite seed exclusions. It is neither a new global upper bound nor evidence of a longer path. The reachability of 21 is open.
