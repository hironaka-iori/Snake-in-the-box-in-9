# Review of accepted milestone 170 closure and classification sources

Reviewed source files: ComponentClosure170.lean, PairSupport170.lean, ComponentClassification170.lean. The coordinating agent reports PASS receipts for all three. This worker inspected their statements and proofs without invoking a compiler.

## Assessment

No assumption gap was found between the accepted coordinate-cut statements and the corresponding part of the written component argument. The cut hypotheses are sufficient without assuming that either coordinate set is connected. This is a useful strengthening of the intermediate theorem, rather than an omitted premise.

The hypotheses are: an actual Snake9.Snake m; absence of all selected unit vertices; nonempty disjoint coordinate sets A and B; and selection of every pair crossing the boundary of A, and every pair crossing the boundary of B. The cut conditions concern each set and its entire complement. Merely assuming all A-B cross-pairs selected would be weaker and would not justify the proof.

## Closure implementation

Cross records actual pair vertices with one coordinate in each set. Family contains Cross together with common neighbors of two distinct Cross vertices. This broader definition avoids an explicit enumeration of candidate triples. It does not assume that every Family vertex is selected.

In the cross-pair branch, a selected neighbor is a triple with three distinct coordinates because units are absent. If its third coordinate lies outside both sets, the two cut hypotheses give three selected pair-side neighbors, contradicting the path degree bound. Otherwise the triple has two distinct Cross neighbors and therefore belongs to Family.

In the common-neighbor branch, its two distinct Cross neighbors are selected by cutA. The path degree bound forces any additional selected neighbor to equal one of them. This establishes closure only on actual selected vertices, which is exactly the generic path-closure theorem's premise.

A Cross vertex supplies a selected witness in Family. Consequently all_selected_in_family applies actual path connectivity. No minimum-length hypothesis is required at this stage because the selected witness and cut assumptions already supply the necessary selected configuration. Omitting m >= 1 from these intermediate statements does not weaken soundness.

## Pair support and classification

PairSupport170 now derives bit support algebraically from testBit identities. Parity is additive under XOR; only the nine unit parity cases and a sixteen-case Boolean rearrangement use finite decide. The former draft's 81/729-tuple parity tables have been removed. The earlier review/pair-support.md describes that superseded draft and should be updated when the milestone is finalized.

selected_pair_cross excludes the common-neighbor alternative by nonadjacency of any two pair vertices. Unordered pair-support uniqueness then gives full coverage by A and B and absence of every distinct within-set pair vertex. Swapping A and B proves the second completeness assertion. These statements exactly express a two-clique missing-pair graph once the cut premises have been instantiated.

## Remaining boundaries

The three files do not yet define missing-pair graph components or prove that component sets satisfy the cuts. That requires a symmetric missing-edge relation, reachability, disjointness and nonemptiness of two distinct reachability classes, and the implication that an absent pair would join their classes. With those supplied, these accepted theorems imply that a disconnected missing-pair graph has exactly two components and each is a clique.

The source does not yet prove the numerical edge lower bound eight, the exact disconnected-case lengths, translation from zero to arbitrary centers, or the global loss implication. Those remain separate assembly and counting steps. In particular, identifying missing-pair count with empty-square count also needs zero itself absent; for the original geometric claim that follows from nontriviality and absence of selected neighbors.

Conclusion: the accepted statements provide the actual-cube closure and cut-classification portion of the written theorem with sufficient explicit premises. The root's reachability layer is the correct next dependency.

## Follow-up: accepted reachability and physical-square bridge

Reviewed MissingComponents170.lean and EmptySquareBridge170.lean after the coordinating agent reported successful Lean checks. No compiler was invoked by this reviewer.

MissingEdge is the actual off-diagonal absent-pair relation: distinct coordinates and an unselected pair vertex. MissingReach is reflexive finite reachability generated by those edges. reach_cut derives selection across a reachability-class boundary directly: an absent crossing pair would extend a missing-edge walk. complement_reach_cut uses symmetry of pair vertices to obtain the opposite cut. Thus the previous cut-instantiation dependency is resolved in the source.

For a root with an unreachable outside coordinate, disconnected_missing_graph_exact characterizes every off-diagonal edge by equality of reachability-class membership. Both parts are nonempty, because root is reachable from itself and outside is assumed unreachable. Thus the edge set is exactly two disjoint cliques, with no missing cross-edges. The complement need not initially be assumed to be a single component: its proved clique property establishes that conclusion, including the singleton case. No additional connectivity premise is hidden in the statement. The theorem is conditioned on a disconnected witness; the connected-versus-disconnected dichotomy is an immediate classical consequence but is not separately packaged there.

translated_units_absent derives the actual absent-unit premise for the XOR-translated snake from absence of any selected neighbor at the original center q. translated_disconnected_missing_graph_exact then states the exact edge classification for that translated actual snake. The arbitrary-center classification dependency is therefore resolved.

in_translate_iff identifies translated selection with original selection at the corresponding XOR-shifted vertex. undominated_center_absent uses the explicit hypothesis m >= 1 to exclude selection of q itself. empty_square_missing_edge_iff then equates absence of all four physical corner vertices q, q xor unit(a), q xor unit(b), q xor pair(a,b) with the translated MissingEdge relation for a != b. The nontriviality condition is present precisely where it is needed. No assumption that q is absent is silently imported.

## Updated remaining dependencies

The actual-geometry statements now cover cuts, disconnected edge classification, arbitrary-center translation, and physical four-corner emptiness. Remaining work for the numerical result is finite counting: connected graphs on nine vertices have at least eight edges; two nonempty clique parts have at least sixteen edges; and the chosen empty-square counting representation corresponds to unordered coordinate pairs. If ordered pairs are used internally, division by two or a canonical a < b enumeration must be accounted for explicitly.

The exact disconnected-case path lengths and their realizations also remain separate from these accepted statements. The global loss identity, nonnegativity of its remaining terms, and a packaged implication L <= 95 implies domination are not proved by these two files. Accordingly the physical-square equivalence can now be described as Lean-checked, while e(q) >= 8 and the loss threshold retain their separate numerical-assembly boundary until those steps are checked.
