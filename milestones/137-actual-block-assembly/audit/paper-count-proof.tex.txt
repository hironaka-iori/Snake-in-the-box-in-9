A consecutive-block construction gives the stronger estimate. Sort the
sampled starts and group consecutive gaps of at most two. Each block has
one common coordinate. Its expanded interval runs from its first start to
three positions after its last start. These intervals cover the original
windows and, in their order, overlap in at most one position.

\begin{lemma}[Improved count for one endpoint]\label{lem:twelve-endpoint-windows}
Under the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:sixteen-endpoint-windows}, there are
at most twelve starts.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the same normalization and sampled starts. Let $W$ be their number,
$k$ the number of blocks, and $A$ the common coordinates used. Put $a=|A|$.
For $W>0$, we have $1\le a\le k$ and $a\le8$. Let $F$ be the absent
coordinate pairs among the eight available coordinates that meet $A$, with
$t=|F|$. Retain the incident and crossing degrees $d_u,s_u$ above.

An interval containing $w$ starts has at least $w+3$ positions. Summing
over the blocks and accounting for at most $k-1$ overlaps gives a covered
union of at least $W+2k+1$ positions. Except at sampled positions zero and
one, these are distinct selected coordinate pairs meeting $A$. The $t$
absent pairs cannot occur among them. Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:missing-capacity-twelve}
W+2k+t\le7a-\binom a2+1.
\end{equation}

For each block, remove sampled positions zero and one when present, and
let $\ell$ be the remaining length. These exceptional positions belong
only to the first block, so their total contribution is at most two.
Every block originally contains at least four positions, hence $\ell\ge2$,
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regular-sum-twelve}
W+3k\le\sum_{\text{blocks}}\ell+2.
\end{equation}
If its common coordinate is $u$, its consecutive regular vertices are
$\{u,b_1\},\ldots,\{u,b_\ell\}$. The $b_i$ are distinct, and each
successive pair of them is absent by Lemma~\ref{lem:missing-opposite-pair}.
Exactly $8-a-s_u$ coordinates outside $A$ can occur in this list: each
outside coordinate gives either a selected pair through $u$ or one of
the $s_u$ absent crossing pairs. The list bound above therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regular-degree-twelve}
\ell+d_u\le\max\{1,8-a-s_u\}+t.
\end{equation}

For $a\le7$, the maximum is at most $8-a$. Every common coordinate occurs
in at least one block. Repeated common coordinates can only increase the
sum of their nonnegative degrees, so $\sum_{\text{blocks}}d_u\ge t$.
Summing \eqref{eq:regular-degree-twelve} and using
\eqref{eq:regular-sum-twelve} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:degree-sum-twelve}
W+3k+t\le k(8-a+t)+2.
\end{equation}
Suppose $W\ge13$. Inequality~\eqref{eq:missing-capacity-twelve} first gives
$k\le8$. Checking the integer ranges $1\le a\le7$, $a\le k\le8$ and
$0\le t\le7a-\binom a2-2k-12$ in
\eqref{eq:degree-sum-twelve} leaves only $(a,k,t)=(5,5,3)$.
This finite implication is checked in Lean.

In that remaining case, each common coordinate occurs in exactly one block.
Since $0\le s_u\le3$, inequality~\eqref{eq:regular-degree-twelve} implies
\[
\ell+d_u+s_u\le
\begin{cases}7,&s_u=3,\\6,&s_u<3.\end{cases}
\]
At most one coordinate has $s_u=3$, and $\sum_{u\in A}(d_u+s_u)=6$.
Thus the sum of the five regular lengths is at most $5\cdot6+1-6=25$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:regular-sum-twelve} gives $W+15\le27$, a contradiction.

If $a=8$, inequality~\eqref{eq:missing-capacity-twelve}, together with
$k\ge8$ and $W\ge13$, forces $k=8$ and $t=0$. Equation
\eqref{eq:regular-degree-twelve} then gives $\ell\le1$ for every block,
contrary to $\ell\ge2$. The case $W=0$ is immediate. The construction,
all counts and their application to actual snakes have passed Lean~\cite{count24archive}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Improved number of low-pair windows]\label{thm:twenty-four-windows}
Every snake in $\Q_9$ has at most twenty-four fitting canonical $012032$
windows with $\alpha_{r+2}+\alpha_{r+4}\le9$. Windows may overlap, and
each starting position is counted once. No parity assumption is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the disjoint endpoint assignment from Theorem~\ref{thm:thirty-two-windows}
and apply Lemma~\ref{lem:twelve-endpoint-windows} to each group.
The full endpoint transfer and partition have passed Lean~\cite{count24archive}.
\end{proof}

The sharper proposed count of eleven at an endpoint with no counted boundary
window remains under verification. The common unselected neighbour is still
part of the per-endpoint assignment hypothesis. No sharpness assertion is made.
The occurrence count alone gives no new upper bound on snake length;
Section~\ref{sec:occurrence-credit-limit} identifies an obstruction to its
use in the current uniform-credit comparison.
