## Repeated changes of the smaller class

Start with a 96-edge arm in the eight-dimensional cube. Keep at least 47 of the 48 vertices in its smaller parity class. Choose its other vertices, endpoints and traversal freely, requiring the result to have 96 edges. At each step there are $1+48(128-48)=3{,}841$ candidate smaller classes. Repeating this move gives the following exact results, with paths identified under translation, permutation of coordinates and reversal.

| Starting arms | Shapes reachable in any number of moves | Largest distance between two shapes, in moves | Result for a join with an arbitrary opposite arm |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Five parents known to reach 191 | 6 | 2 | Maximum exactly 191. Five attain it. The sixth gives at most 190. |
| Round 254 arm `e6e522b8` | 41 | 5 | 181 is attained. Twelve shapes exclude 192 at both ends. The other 29 remain open. |

The six shapes are exactly those reached by the earlier operation of retaining 68 consecutive edges and regrowing both ends. One may alternate the two operations indefinitely from any of the five attaining parents and still stay within these six shapes. The two operations produce different outcomes away from that component. In particular, 23 of the 41 shapes are outside the previous 241-shape inventory. Distances from `e6e522b8` give successive counts of 1, 7, 14, 13 and 6. In both components every successful replacement changes exactly two coordinate bits of the replaced vertex. For a proposed smaller class, two of its vertices may be consecutive in the contracted traversal precisely when an opposite-parity vertex has exactly those two neighbours in the class. An endpoint attachment has just one neighbour there. A 96-edge arm needs a spanning traversal of the 48 fixed vertices, one connecting label at each of its 47 links, and an attachment at both ends. These choices give an induced path because the prescribed neighbour sets exclude every cross-parity chord. Each enumeration considered all orders and labels. One searched forward along the traversal. The other represented the attachments by edges to an auxiliary vertex and selected a connected degree-two graph spanning all 49 vertices. Both reproduced the outgoing shapes and multiplicities at every parent. A finite catalogue closed under every move contains every shape reachable by repetition, and the saved discovery paths give the converse. ## Why the overlap bound applies to every segment

For the distant reference, only four of the 3,840 strict replacements work. They are $17\mapsto177$, $83\mapsto209$, $132\mapsto149$ and $216\mapsto80$. The first gives four labelled arms. Each of the others gives one. Retaining the original class gives one more arm. All eight represent different shapes. The three new shapes from the first replacement use independent choices between vertices 2 and 16 and between 25 and 155, together with a fourth, previously known shape. The new shape from the last replacement admits the full saved 84-edge partner at vertex 161 and gives a verified 181-edge join. [All eight paths and exchanges](geometry.json) · [Explicit constructions](constructive.json)

Four of the eight shapes had earlier endpoint exclusions. For the four new ones, a 192-edge join needs a 95-edge partner. The first proof search treated the whole opposite face and, where necessary, each possible first edge. A further search reused these certificates among neighbouring parents. If a proposed partner takes a first step excluded for a former parent, it must visit a vertex freed by changing that parent. Otherwise the same partner would be legal for the former parent. All eight new endpoint exclusions were replayed, with 16 direct contradiction archives and five additional archives using the earlier exclusions. A separate bounded search with four-dimensional occupancy constraints timed out on all eight full queries. If it is shorter than the arm, the preceding or following edge in that face extends the segment. The direct cube checks excluded every such extension at both ends of all 47 shapes in the two components. ## Constructing the opposite arm

The 25 saved 94-edge partners of the five attaining parents give six distinct labelled sets of 48 even vertices after translation to the crossing. Retaining or exchanging one vertex gives 22,782 distinct candidate sets after overlaps between the six families are removed. A 95-edge partner has one attachment to its fixed 48 vertices, with the opposite endpoint on the fixed side. Two enumerations produced exactly 230 labelled paths on 60 viable sets, representing 72 shapes. We placed every consecutive segment of each of the 72 shapes against both ends of all 264 catalogued long arms, in both directions and under all $8!$ coordinate permutations. The maximum was **191**, attained at five rooted parents. In the 41-shape component this particular partner collection attained a maximum of **133**. An 181-edge join there uses a partner outside the collection. Every reported maximum has an explicit ordered witness and a separate check rejecting any segment one edge longer. The enlarged 264-shape inventory has 171 shapes whose unrestricted partners could still give 192. Other long arms and paths crossing between the two faces more than once also remain possible.
