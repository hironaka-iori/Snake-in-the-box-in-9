\subsection{Subset forest inequalities and their remaining limitation}
\label{sec:forest220}
For any vertex subset $U$, an induced path restricts to a forest.
Writing $E(U)$ for allowed edges with both endpoints in $U$, its Boolean
vertex and induced-edge indicators therefore satisfy, for every $v\in U$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forest220}
\sum_{ab\in E(U)}y_{ab}\le\sum_{u\in U}x_u-x_v.
\end{equation}
If $v$ is selected, the nonempty forest has at least one component and
at most one fewer edge than vertices. If $v$ is unselected, the weaker
bound follows, including when no vertex is selected.

Lean derives the counting bound directly from an indexed induced path.
Every selected ambient edge joins consecutive indices. Given a marked
selected index, assign each edge its endpoint farther from that index.
These assigned indices are distinct and avoid the marked index. This
injection proves the one-unit saving, with the empty case handled separately.
The concrete variable and edge-enumeration interpretation remains separate.

\paragraph{Separation by endpoint flow.}
Put $\eta_t=1$ at the root and $\eta_u=e_u$ elsewhere. Summing degree
equations over $U$ gives
\[
2\sum_{u\in U}x_u-\sum_{u\in U}\eta_u
=2\sum_{ab\in E(U)}y_{ab}+\sum_{ab\in\delta(U)}y_{ab},
\]
where $\delta(U)$ consists of edges leaving $U$. Hence
\eqref{eq:forest220} is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forest-cut220}
\sum_{ab\in\delta(U)}y_{ab}+\sum_{u\in U}\eta_u\ge2x_v.
\end{equation}
The signed algebraic equivalence is checked in Lean under the displayed
degree identity. Give each original undirected edge capacity $y_{ab}$
and add an edge of capacity $\eta_u$ from each vertex to a new sink.
A minimum cut from $v$ to the sink finds a most violated subset for $v$.

The round-219 rational points violate this stronger condition.
The two new computations each add 25 explicit subset inequalities.
Exact weighted-row certificates then give
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{Root}&\text{Exact feasible total weight}&\text{Exact upper bound}\\\hline
0&\displaystyle\frac{22588258102073}{230867380070}&97.840921\\[6pt]
78&\displaystyle\frac{3545809907526}{36419686519}&97.359759
\end{array}
\]
The feasible values are approximately $97.840839$ and $97.359704$.
Both upper bounds are below 98. An actual odd-edge partner has an even
number of vertices, so it has at most 96 vertices and 95 edges.
In particular, both 97-edge partner targets are excluded. Any partner
with at least 97 edges would have such a prefix, so arbitrary partners
at these roots have at most 96 edges. A one-crossing join using this
particular 98-edge parent consequently has at most 195 edges.
This recovers the earlier native 97-edge exclusions with explicit exact
weighted inequalities. It does not cover other parents or arrangements.

\paragraph{Why the complete subset family still permits the target.}
Each exact rational point in the table satisfies every cycle occupancy
inequality by the deleted-edge potentials of Section~\ref{sec:linear219}.
For every vertex $v$, a separate feasible flow to the new sink carries
at least $2x_v$. Summing flow conservation over any $U$ containing $v$
bounds this supply by its outward cut capacity, proving
\eqref{eq:forest-cut220} for every such $U$ simultaneously.
The exact audit verifies all 158 flow systems at each root, their 832
directed arc capacities, conservation equations and degree identities.
No numerical maximum-flow optimality assertion is needed for this deduction.

Mixing either point with the corresponding one-edge path again gives
total selection weight exactly 94. Convexity preserves every cycle and
subset inequality. Thus the complete stated continuous formulation
still cannot exclude the 93-edge partner target. The flows and numerical
arrays have independent exact audits; the generic Lean implications and
actual-path forest bound do not constitute an end-to-end numerical proof.
All data and proof receipts are retained in~\cite{research220}.

\subsection{The next missing constraint: consistency of edge products}
\label{sec:product220}
For Boolean vertex selections and their edge products, put
$d_{uv}=x_u+x_v-2y_{uv}$. This is one exactly when the two selections
differ. A closed cycle $C$ has an even number of such changes. Therefore,
for any odd-sized subset $F$ of its edges,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cut-parity220}
\sum_{e\in F}d_e-\sum_{e\in E(C)\setminus F}d_e\le |F|-1.
\end{equation}
Indeed, matching the odd pattern $F$ on every edge would violate even
parity; at least one mismatch is necessary. These are classical cycle
inequalities for the cut polytope~\cite{barahona1986}. Their use here
concerns consistency of the selected-edge products, rather than the
number of selected vertices on a cycle.

The new root-0 fractional point assigns the following disagreement values
to a coordinate square:
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
44&\overset{0}{\longleftrightarrow}&45\\
0\,\updownarrow&&\updownarrow\,1\\
60&\underset{0}{\longleftrightarrow}&61.
\end{array}
\]
Three zero disagreements require all four Boolean selections to agree,
contradicting the remaining value one. The square violates
\eqref{eq:cut-parity220} by one, although the same fractional point
satisfies every forest and occupancy inequality. The exact-weight-94
mixture also violates it. An independent audit finds square violations
at root 78 as well. These are concrete reasons to strengthen the model.
The two square parity forms have a separate Boolean Lean check;
the general cycle assertion has the elementary proof above.
No strengthened linear program containing these parity rows has yet
been solved in this round, and no improved bound is inferred from them.
