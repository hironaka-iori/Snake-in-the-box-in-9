\begin{lemma}[Six empty squares at an undominated corner]
\label{lem:six-empty-undominated}
For a nontrivial snake in $\Q_9$, $d(q)=0$ implies $e(q)\ge6$.
Consequently, $\sum_q r_{12}(q)\le71$ implies that every cube vertex
has a selected neighbour.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Translate $q$ to zero and let $H$ be the simple graph on the nine
coordinate directions whose edges are the missing weight-two vertices.
Zero and all weight-one vertices are unselected, so $|E(H)|=e(q)$.
The argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:undominated-corners} excludes two isolated
vertices of $H$. Suppose that $|E(H)|\le5$.

If $H$ has an isolated vertex $c$, the other eight vertices have positive
degree and total degree at most ten. At least six therefore have degree
one. For each such direction $a$, with unique partner $a'$, the selected
pair $\{c,a\}$ has only the possible selected neighbour $\{c,a,a'\}$.
Minimum degree forces that neighbour. Three of these distinct pair
vertices already contradict the two-endpoint property.

Thus $H$ has no isolated vertex. Its degree sum lies between nine and
ten and is even, so it equals ten. Exactly one direction has degree two,
and eight have degree one. Hence $H$ consists of a three-vertex path and
three isolated edges.

Choose two isolated edges $\{a,a'\}$ and $\{b,b'\}$. Their four cross-pair
vertices are selected. Each can have selected neighbours only among the
four triples obtained by adjoining the partner of one coordinate;
any other triple would have three selected pair neighbours. These four
pairs and four possible triples form an eight-cycle. An included triple
already has two selected pair neighbours, so it cannot have a selected
weight-four neighbour. The selected part of this eight-cycle therefore
has no selected edge leading outside it.

At least one triple is absent, since a path contains no cycle. All four
pair vertices retain a neighbour, by minimum degree. The resulting
nonempty union of paths has at least two degree-one vertices, which are
endpoints of the whole snake. The three choices of two isolated edges
give disjoint sets of pair and triple vertices, since their coordinate
supports identify the two chosen edges. They force at least six distinct
endpoints, a contradiction. Thus $e(q)\ge6$.

Finally, a degree-zero corner contributes $12e(q)\ge72$ to a sum of
nonnegative remainders. This proves the loss implication.
\end{proof}

This strengthening is a written geometric proof. An exhaustive integer
check of all 443704 coordinate graphs with at most five edges leaves
3780 graphs after the two endpoint restrictions; all have the stated
three-vertex-path and three-isolated-edge structure. A separate check of
the 16 possible triple selections verifies the eight-cycle endpoint
count. These finite checks support the proof but do not constitute its
complete Lean verification, which remains pending. The strengthened
domination threshold alone gives no new numerical length bound.
