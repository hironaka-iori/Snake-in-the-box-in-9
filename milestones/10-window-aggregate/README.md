# Window exclusions and stronger aggregate bounds

Checkpoint: 19 October 2026.

**Status.** Three new *conditional* upper bounds are proved in Lean: at most **242 edges**, at most **241 edges** with one extra premise, at most **239 edges** from a direction-aware automaton with 145 certified window exclusions, at most **238 edges** with five-centre windows (325 exclusions), at most **235 edges** with six-centre windows (2,635 exclusions), and at most **233 edges** with six-centre windows and counts lumped at eight instead of six (8,182 exclusions). The premises are exclusions of certain singleton-count patterns on three or four consecutive internal path vertices. Each premise has a **DRAT-checked** unsatisfiability certificate for an explicit local CNF. None is a Lean theorem yet. The unconditional checked interval therefore remains **191 <= a(9) <= 245**.

## What this milestone adds

The incidence identity gives `T + 2*s0 = 9214 - 35*v`. Here `T` is the total singleton-square count, `s0` the number of empty squares, and `v` the number of vertices. The existing proof bounds `T` from below by a potential argument on the count sequence `alpha_0, ..., alpha_{v-1}` along the path. Its worst sequences repeat `2,2,3`, with average `7/3`. Because a universal bound `alpha >= 2` is false, any improvement must restrict *how often* low counts occur.

This milestone asks which count windows a local ball model allows. It then derives the best aggregate inequality those windows support.

1. **Local model** (`scripts/wincert.py`). Fix consecutive path vertices `x_0..x_{k+1}` with internal centers `x_1..x_k` and prescribed counts. The model is the radius-three Hamming ball around the centers. Every clause is a necessary condition on `S ∩ B` for an induced path `S`:
   - the window vertices are selected;
   - every ball vertex has at most two selected neighbours;
   - a vertex whose whole neighbourhood lies in the ball has one or two, and one means endpoint;
   - at most two endpoints occur;
   - each center's count equals its prescribed value: count the absent opposite corners over the 21 coordinate pairs avoiding its path directions;
   - there are no 4-cycles, no 6-cycles inside 3-faces, and no cycle cuts found lazily.

   Up to coordinate relabelling and reversal, there are two 3-center window shapes, with directions `0,1,2,0` and `0,1,2,3`. There are three 4-center shapes: `0,1,2,0,3`, `0,1,2,3,0`, and `0,1,2,3,4`.
2. **Survey** (`scripts/survey.py`, `data/survey_*.json`). Every 3-center count triple with entries 1..6 was decided in both shapes. CaDiCaL with this encoding and an independent CP-SAT model agree on all 432 cases. Of the 216 triples, 144 are feasible in some shape. The minimum mean count over sequences whose consecutive triples are feasible is **14/5**. The worst cycle is `2,3,3,2,4`. The current Lean premises give **7/3**.
3. **Ceiling with four windows** (`data/survey_c4_r3_k5.json`). Every count quadruple in 1..5 was decided in each 4-center shape whose 3-center subwindows are feasible: 654 cases, 293 infeasible. A window `x_0..x_5` with counts `(2,4,2,3)` or `(3,2,4,2)` is infeasible in all three 4-center shapes, and adding only this exclusion raises the minimum mean to **3**. With the complete table the shape-blind minimum mean is still exactly 3, since for example `(3,3,3,3)` is feasible in shape `0,1,2,3,0`. So **241 edges is the limit of shape-blind radius-three windows of length at most four.**
4. **Certificates** (`scripts/certify.py`, `certificates/`, `data/certificates.json`). There are 22 cases:
   - 16 for the triples, in both shapes and both orientations: `(2,2,3)`, `(2,2,4)`, `(2,3,2)`, `(3,2,3)`, and `(4,1,c)` with `4<=c<=6`;
   - 6 for `(2,4,2,3)` and its reversal.

   CaDiCaL produced each DRUP proof. The repository's vendored `drat-trim` verified every one (`work/keep96/vendor/drat-trim`, source hash checked). The gzipped CNF and proof files are about 19 MB.
5. **Positive controls** (`scripts/controls.py`, `data/controls.json`). There are 532 windows: from the 131 bank snakes, the 191-edge witness, and the 137-edge count-one counterexample, including counts 1 and 3. Each satisfies every clause of its CNF with its true counts and selection.
6. **Lean.**
   - `work/spectral/stronger/WindowPotential242.lean` and `WindowPotential241.lean` use core Lean only. They prove the sequence inequalities `14*n <= 5*sum + 12` and `3*n <= sum + 4` by potentials checked by kernel evaluation of finite tables.
   - `AggregateWindowConditional.lean` uses mathlib and combines them with the existing incidence identity. It proves `upper_242_of_certified_windows` and `upper_241_of_certified_windows` for the original `Snake9.Snake`.
   - The (2,2,2) case is discharged by the existing theorem, and the count-one neighbour bound and endpoint bound are reused. Only the listed window premises remain as hypotheses.
   - *Reproduction:* a fresh source-only run of `verify_strengthened.py` succeeded. It covered 156 steps: core, incidence theorem, every strengthening module including these three, and the final audit. Both conditional theorems, and the unconditional 245 interval, audit to `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound` only. The manifest and combined log are `outputs/strengthened-window-reproduction-manifest.json` and `outputs/strengthened-window-reproduction.log`. The run used `--core-targets Snake9 PathStructure CubeLocal SquareCounterexample SingletonSquareLemma OptimalityTarget`, the only core modules the chain imports.

7. **Independent judge** (`scripts/judge.py`, `data/judge.json`). This is a high-level decision procedure that reuses none of the generating code. Its verdict is **ACCEPT**:
   - (A) It re-encodes every premise from the definitions, with a different variable order and a different cardinality encoding. It checks the recorded cycle cuts are genuine cycles, refutes each formula with Glucose, and verifies each proof with `drat-trim`. All 22 are verified.
   - (B) It classifies every vertex-only clause of the stored certificates semantically as a window unit, a degree-3 star, an interior-neighbour clause, a 4- or 6-cycle of a face, or a cycle cut. None is unclassified.
   - (C) It normalises 4,979 windows of real snakes, every window with a count at most 4 plus a sample. Each satisfies the judge's encoding with its true counts.
   - (D) An independent dynamic program reproduces 245 for the proved premises and 244 for the existing (2,2,3) condition, and gives 242 and 241 for the new premise sets.

   This gives two independent implementations of the encoding, but it is still a test of the bridge, not a proof.

## Direction-aware automaton: conditional bound 239

The shape-blind ceiling above is 3 (241 edges). Tracking which coordinates the path uses breaks it, because the only low-count windows that survive at radius three are tied to particular transition shapes, and consecutive windows of those shapes cannot be chained for long.

**Abstraction** (`scripts/refine.py`). A state is the canonical shape of the last six transition directions, up to relabelling (12 induced shapes), together with the last three lumped counts (`min(alpha,6)`, 216 combinations): 2592 states. A transition appends one direction label and one count. It is allowed unless the seven-direction block is not an induced path or some window of two, three or four consecutive centres ending at the new vertex is known to be infeasible in the radius-three local model. Unknown windows are allowed, so the automaton over-approximates real snakes and its minimum cycle mean is a valid lower bound on the average internal count given the exclusions it uses.

**Refinement.** Howard's policy iteration finds a minimum-mean cycle; the windows on it are sent to the SAT oracle; infeasible ones are added. With six directions of memory the mean rises from 8/3 to **10/3** in five rounds (four or five directions stall at 3; seven add nothing). With five-centre windows and seven directions it reaches **7/2**.

**Minimal exclusions** (`scripts/minimize.py`). Of 333 relevant infeasible windows, dual slack pruning keeps the mean 10/3 and the integer potential (range 8) with **145**; an exact-greedy pass over the vertex bound reaches 93 (mean 23/7, same bound by dynamic programming). The potential route uses the 145 set:

| route | exclusions | mean | potential range | edge bound |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L=6, k=4 (potential, Lean) | 145 | 10/3 | 8 | **239** |
| L=6, k=4 (exact DP) | 93 | 23/7 | — | 239 |
| L=7, k=5 (potential, Lean) | 325 | 7/2 | 6 | **238** |
| L=7, k=6 (potential, Lean) | 2635 | 4 | 6 | **235** |

Arithmetic for 239: the potential gives `3 Σ_{j=6}^{m-1} min(alpha_j,6) ≥ 10 (m-6) - 8`; with `alpha_1..alpha_5 ≥ 1` and endpoints at least four, `3 T ≥ 10 m - 36`; with `T ≤ 9214 - 35 (m+1)` this is `115 m ≤ 27573`, so `m ≤ 239`.

**Certificates** (`scripts/certify_direction.py`, `data/direction_certificates.json`, `certificates/direction/*.cnf.gz`). Every one of the 145 windows has a drat-trim-verified refutation. The CNF files are committed; the DRUP proofs (250 MB) are not, their SHA256 is recorded and the script regenerates them. `scripts/judge.py --index` re-encodes all 145 independently and refutes them with Glucose: 145 verified (one case needed a solo rerun because the batch hit the checker's time limit under CPU contention; `data/judge_direction.json`). `scripts/direction_bound.py` rebuilds the automaton from the certificate index alone, checks the integer potential on every one of the 29,559 allowed edges and recomputes the exact bound (`data/direction_bound_L6_k4.json`). The 325-window set for 7/2 is certified the same way (`data/direction_certificates_k5.json`, all 325 verified; `data/direction_bound_L7_k5.json` re-checks its potential and bound; the independent judge re-encodes and refutes all 325, `data/judge_direction_k5.json`). Certifying it became practical only after the lazy cycle-cut loop was made incremental (one CaDiCaL instance per window instead of a cold restart per cut): the remaining 219 windows then took minutes instead of hours.

**Lean.** Six modules in `work/spectral/stronger/`:

- `WindowAutomatonDefs.lean` (core Lean): first-occurrence relabelling `canon`, restricted growth strings, the parity test `inducedPattern` (a block of directions must not have all multiplicities even, nor exactly one odd when it has two or more steps), enumeration helpers.
- `WindowAutomatonData.lean` (generated by `scripts/gen_lean_automaton.py`): the exclusion table and the potential as bitmask literals, the step test `check`, and `step_table : allRGS 7 0 check = true`, which the kernel evaluates in about a minute (877 restricted growth strings times 1296 count combinations). The generator re-implements `check` in Python and asserts it on the same 29,559 transitions before writing the file.
- `WindowAutomatonLemmas.lean` (core): `canon` is invariant under injective relabelling, as is `inducedPattern`; so the table applies to an arbitrary seven-direction window (`step_raw`).
- `WindowAutomatonSequence.lean` (core): the telescoped inequality `total_bound : 10 m ≤ 3 Σ_{j=1}^{m-1} alpha_j + 60`.
- `WindowAutomatonBridge.lean` (core, imports the standard-library core): `pathDir` extracts the flipped coordinate of each edge; the XOR of a block of unit vectors has bit `b` equal to the parity of the count of `b`, so `distinct` and `induced` of `Snake9.Snake` give `inducedPattern` for every window.
- `AggregateDirectionConditional.lean` (mathlib): `upper_239_of_direction_windows`. Its only premise is that no two-, three- or four-centre window of the snake has a direction shape and lumped counts listed in `forbMask`; everything else is proved.

The five-centre automaton (34 shapes, 44,064 states) is handled the same way by `WindowAutomatonK5R3Data/Table/Sequence.lean` and `AggregateDirectionK5R3Conditional.lean` (`upper_238_of_direction_windowsK5R3`). Its step table is too large for one kernel evaluation (a single file was killed after 12 minutes), so `scripts/gen_lean_automaton_k.py` emits one module per canonical 7-shape, `WindowAutomatonK5R3Shard0..33.lean` (the original `allSym`-nested shards took minutes each and were regenerated with the `letN` loop design of the six-centre chain: about 20 s per shard), and a kernel-checked membership lemma shows every induced restricted growth string of length eight is a listed shape followed by a label below nine (`checkK5R3_of_rgs`). Arithmetic: `2 Σ_{j=7}^{m-1} min(alpha_j,6) ≥ 7 (m-7) - 6`, so `2 T ≥ 7 m - 21 - 6` and `77 m ≤ 18385`, giving `m ≤ 238`.

All of these audit to `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`. They are included in `verify_strengthened.py`; a fresh 200-step source-only run (core, incidence, every strengthening module including the 34 shards, final audit) is recorded in `outputs/strengthened-direction-reproduction-manifest.json` and `outputs/strengthened-direction-reproduction.log`.

## Six-centre windows: conditional bound 235

With seven directions of memory and windows of up to six centres (34 shapes × 6^5 counts = 264,384 states, up to 2.7 million allowed transitions) the refinement loop reaches mean **4** after 186 rounds (`data/refine_L7_k6_K5_r3.json`). Batching the windows of every near-minimal cycle of Howard's policy graph, not only the minimum one, is what made the later rounds productive. The vectorised minimiser keeps **2,635** windows (3 two-, 60 three-, 314 four-, 2,074 five- and 184 six-centre), with an integer potential of range 6 (`data/minimal_L7_k6_K5_r3_fix.json`). An earlier set of 2,579 windows (`minimal_L7_k6_K5_r3_cheap.json`) was unsound as a premise list: eleven of its windows came from the three-centre survey seeds with an *exact* count 6 at one position, and the table builder mapped an exact 6 to the lumped symbol `>= 6`. A certificate for `alpha = 6` does not exclude `alpha >= 7`; re-deciding those eleven with the range `[6, 21]` leaves three of them feasible, and without them the same tables give only 7/2 (238). `refine_fast.forb_tables` and `minimize_fast.sym_codes` now map a certified range to exactly the symbols whose own ranges it contains, the refinement was rerun from the cache and reached mean 4 again, and every window of the new set is stated with the lumped range it is used with. The 239 and 238 sets contain no such window. Four high-count four-centre windows of the first minimal set (`data/avoid_k6.json`) had refutations that did not finish in hours; the loop was rerun with those treated as feasible and a 60-second limit per oracle query, and reached mean 4 again with cheap substitutes, so every window in the final set certifies in at most a minute. Arithmetic: `Σ_{j=7}^{m-1} min(alpha_j,6) ≥ 4 (m-7) - 6`, so `T ≥ 4 m - 26` and `39 m ≤ 9199`, giving `m ≤ 235`; the exact dynamic programme gives the same 235. The kernel check needed a rewrite of the enumeration (`work/spectral/stronger/WindowAutomatonLoop.lean`). Measurements on one (shape, label) pair of 46,656 transitions: the original closure-nested loop over symbols took 400 s and 7 GB; a flat 46,656-deep structural recursion overflows the kernel stack; `decide` on a `Nat` inequality costs about 0.3 ms per use and every bit test on a table of 46,656 bits allocates the whole shifted table, because the kernel substitutes expressions and does not share the value of a closed subterm across loop iterations. The working design is a `Nat.rec` loop that tests the body before the recursive call (`allBelowR`, constant stack), tables indexed by the base-6 symbol code, an outer loop over the four oldest symbols and an inner loop over the two newest, and `letN (v + 1) k = k v`, whose major premise forces the kernel to normalise the 36-bit window of each table once per outer step and re-enter it as a numeral; the inner loop then uses only `Nat.beq`, `Nat.ble`, `>>>` and `&&&` on small numerals. One pair now takes 5 s and 0.6 GB, a shard of nine pairs 35-50 s, the 34 shards about ten minutes on three cores. The transport lemmas (`bitT_win`, `fldT_win`) relate windowed bits and 4-bit fields to the original tables through `Nat.testBit`, and a generic extraction lemma proved on abstract tables (`checkT_spec`) keeps every elaborator-side unification away from the concrete loop (a `simp` under the loop binder on the concrete tables made the kernel unroll all 46,656 iterations).

A fresh source-only reproduction (`verify_strengthened.py --build-dir`, resumable across the machine restarts; `outputs/strengthened-direction-k6-reproduction-manifest.json`, 239 steps) re-checked the core, every strengthening module, the three automaton chains (239, the regenerated 238, the sound 235) and the final audit; every bound audits to `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`. All 2,635 windows are drat-trim-certified (`data/direction_certificates_k6.json`); `refine_fast.py --index` rebuilds the automaton from the index alone and re-checks the potential on every allowed edge (`data/direction_bound_L7_k6.json`); the independent judge re-encodes and refutes them (`data/judge_direction_k6.json`: all 2,650 verified, no failures). The Lean chain is produced by `scripts/gen_lean_automaton_k.py` (modules `WindowAutomatonK6R3*`, 34 shards, and `AggregateDirectionK6R3Conditional.lean` with `upper_235_of_direction_windowsK6R3`); the generated potential is defined as `min 6 field`, so its range bound is definitional and the Lean constant matches the dynamic programme. Radius four with four-centre windows gave no gain (10/3, `data/refine_L6_k4_K5_r4.json`).

### SWAR kernel check: 36 transitions per big-integer step

The second kernel design (`work/spectral/stronger/WindowAutomatonSwar.lean`, generator `scripts/gen_lean_swar.py`, modules `WindowAutomatonK6R3W*`, theorem `upper_235_of_direction_windowsK6R3W`) replaces the inner loop over the two newest symbols by arithmetic on packed 8-bit fields ("SIMD within a register", here within one `Nat`). The potentials of a shape are stored as a 7,776-byte number, one byte per symbol label (`potC`), and again in a transposed order (`potT`) in which the 216 labels sharing the three oldest symbols are consecutive; the window tables of two to six centres are spread to one bit per 8-bit field (`sfwd`/`srev`, bit `8 l + 7` of field `l`; tables of fewer centres carry copies so the same field index serves every width). For a shape pair and the three oldest symbols `h` the check of all 216 transitions (newest three symbols) is one `letN` chain: the 216-field windows of the exclusion tables are or-ed together (`BL`), the next-state potentials come out of `potT` as one 1,728-bit window plus `P * ones`, the current-state potentials out of `potC` as a 288-bit window replicated 36 times (`rep`) plus `Q * wpat`, and `swarOK A B BL HI = Nat.beq ((((B + HI) - A) &&& HI) ||| BL) HI` decides `A ≤ B` field-wise with the usual carry trick (`swarOK_spec`, proved for abstract field lists with `pack`/`tab`/`digit`). Soundness is again a generic extraction lemma (`checkT_spec`) on abstract tables; the only per-shape kernel facts are byte bounds (`potC_bnd`, `potT_bnd`), the consistency of the two potential layouts (`potT_cons`) and the copy structure of the spread tables (`sfwd_rep`, `srev_rep`).

Measured on the same four-core cloud machine with three other jobs running (the first column is the `letN` loop design, the second the first SWAR version with a 216-field comparison per outer step, the third the final chain of the reproduction `outputs/strengthened-direction-k6w-reproduction-manifest.json`, which records the seconds of every step):

| step | `letN` loop (K6R3) | SWAR, 216 fields per step | SWAR, one comparison per pair (K6R3W) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| one (shape, label) pair, 46,656 transitions | 5.8 s, 0.6 GB | 0.63 s | about 0.2 s including elaboration |
| all 34 shards | about 25 CPU-minutes | 130 s | 98 s (1.4-4.8 s per module, mostly import and elaboration) |
| `Data` (tables, bounds, layout consistency) | 1 min | 495 s | 97 s |
| `Table` (shape membership) | 142 s | 142 s | 22 s |
| `Spec`, `Sequence`, wrapper, final audit | - | 2 s, 10 s, 1 min, - | 2 s, 7 s, 11 s, 17 s |
| whole 235 chain | about 35 min | about 15 min | about 4 min |

The kernel work per pair fell by nine times. Three further changes attack the rest. (1) Lean parses a decimal literal digit by digit, quadratic in its length: the 347,000-bit spread tables cost four seconds each to *elaborate* (124 s for the data module); they are now written as lists of 8192-bit chunks folded by `ofChunks`, which the kernel rebuilds once per forced `letN` (elaboration 6 s). (2) The 68 per-shape byte-bound theorems (`allBelowR 7776` loops, about 2 s each) became two mask tests per table, `P &&& HI = 0` and `(P + 121 * ONE) &&& HI = 0` with `HI`/`ONE` the top bit and the unit in every field (`bytes_lt_of_masks`): a field below 128 that stays below 128 after adding 121 is at most 6. The data module went from 495 s to 201 s. The 34 layout-consistency loops (`potT_cons`, about 4.5 s each), which compare the natural and transposed potential tables entry by entry, were then restructured block by block: `consT` forces the two 1,728-bit windows of each 216-block to numerals with `letN` before the 216 inner comparisons, so every division is on small numbers; on one shape this took one tenth of the time of the flat loop (`consT_spec` recovers the entry-wise statement through `digit_winT`). (3) The table module spends its time in `inducedPattern`, which recomputes `oddCount` (list filters and counts) for the 36 blocks of each of the 4,140 restricted growth strings; `WindowAutomatonCode.lean` provides `inducedFast`, the same test on xor masks of the direction bits (a block is admissible when its mask is nonzero and, from two steps on, not a power of two), proved to follow from `inducedPattern` (`inducedFast_of_inducedPattern`, through the parity of `List.count` and the distinctness of `firsts`), and a bitset of shape codes with the injectivity of the base-8 code (`codeFrom_inj`) in place of the 34-way list membership. The final step removes the loop altogether: the window tables of `c` centres (`6^c` codes) are replicated to the full `6^6 = 46,656` fields by one multiplication with `repK` (`testBit_mul_rep`), the next-state potentials `potT` likewise (six copies), and the current-state potentials come from a third stored layout `potR` in which every 36-field block of `potC` is repeated six times (checked block by block against `potC`, `consR`). A (shape, label) pair is then **one** field-wise comparison of two 373,000-bit numbers, `swarOK (potT * repA + P * ones) (Q * wpat + potR) BL hi`, and `checkT_spec` reads the transition inequality of every code off it; a shard of nine pairs compiles in a few seconds, most of it import and elaboration overhead. The table module uses `inducedFast` and the shape-code bitset. The same generator gives the five-centre chain `K5R3W` (`upper_238_of_direction_windowsK5R3W`: data module 27 s, 34 shards 86 s, table 54 s on the loaded machine). `verify_strengthened.py` reproduces 238 and 235 through these chains by default (`--k5-chain K5R3`, `--k6-chain K6R3` keep the earlier designs). The run with both SWAR chains (`outputs/strengthened-direction-swar-reproduction-manifest.json`, 243 steps, resumed in the same build directory so the 235 chain is marked cached there; its fresh timings are in `outputs/strengthened-direction-k6w-reproduction-manifest.json`) succeeded, and every bound audits to `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`. Timings are sensitive to the cloud machine's state: in its slow phases a 1.5 GB allocation takes 100 s instead of one, and every kernel evaluation slows by an order of magnitude, so the figures above were taken in a normal phase.

### Faster refinement: Howard's algorithm in C, many cycles per round

At lump cap 7 the six-centre automaton has 1,114,112 states and about 11.6 million live edges. A round of `refine_fast.py` used to take 80-220 s and kill the windows of a single cycle: Howard's policy iteration was a Python loop over every state (76 s of a 90 s round), and its final policy graph had one cycle, so the `--batch` option added nothing. Oracle queries were never the bottleneck (median 0.12 s, 11 timeouts in 12,900 queries). `scripts/howard_c.c` (called through `howard_fast.py`) runs the same evaluation and improvement rules in C: 1.5 s per solve on that graph, the same minimum (89/21 on the same 21-edge cycle), and no extra iterations when warm-started from the previous round's policy. Each round then extracts up to 200 near-minimal cycles by deleting the edges of every cycle found and solving again (200 cycles with means 4.238 to 4.290 and 7,299 distinct edges in under three minutes), and queries all their windows together. Oracle answers are appended to the cache one by one (`refine.Oracle.query`), so a restart of the machine loses no decided window. Every cycle found is a cycle of the true graph and its mean is recomputed exactly; the termination test is unchanged.

## Lump cap 7: conditional bound 233

With symbols `min(alpha, 8)` (counts 1..7 exact, `>= 8` lumped; 1,114,112 states) the six-centre refinement, run with the C Howard and many cycles per round, moved the minimum cycle mean of the cached exclusions from 4.23 to 69/16 in one round and to 48/11 (about 4.364) in the next. The minimum mean alone is not the bound: at 69/16 the potential range is 114 and both the potential bound and the exact walk bound (`vertex_bound`) give 234; at 48/11 (range 83) both give **233** (`scripts/check_bound.py` computes this for the whole cache).

The Lean chain uses the smaller mean 13/3 instead of 48/11 (`minimize_fast.py --lam 13/3`, `gen_lean_swar.py --lam 13/3`): its Bellman-Ford potential has range 20, the bound is still 233, and the fields fit the 8-bit SWAR layout (`P + R <= 128` and `8 Q + R < 128`; at 48/11 they do not). Minimising for 13/3 keeps **8,182** windows (3 two-, 78 three-, 472 four-, 5,725 five- and 1,904 six-centre; `data/minimal_L7_k6_K7_r3.json`); minimising for 48/11 keeps 8,651. Treating the windows that were slowest to refute as feasible breaks the 13/3 potential even for the slowest 326 (minimum mean 29/7), so the hard windows carry the bound. Arithmetic: the sequence lemma gives `13 m <= 3 * sum alpha + 93` (constant `7P - 6Q + R` with `P/Q = 13/3`, `R = 20`), and with `T + 2 s0 = 9214 - 35 v` this gives `m <= 233`.

Certification (`certify_direction.py`) solves each window once with proof logging and checks the DRUP proof with drat-trim; windows that needed lazy cycle cuts are proved again from cold, because a proof extended across clause additions can ask drat-trim to re-add clauses that variable elimination deleted (one such proof was rejected by drat-trim and redone). All 8,182 windows have drat-trim-verified refutations (`data/direction_certificates_k6.json`, now 8,229 verified cases; the CNF and proof files are not kept, their SHA-256 hashes are), and `refine_fast.py --index` rebuilds the automaton from the certified windows alone: minimum mean 13/3, the potential checked on every edge, range 20, at most 233 edges (`data/direction_bound_L7_k6_K7.json`). The Lean chain (`WindowAutomatonK6R3C7W*`, generated from that index, theorem `upper_233_of_direction_windowsK6R3C7W`) is the SWAR design at eight symbols: 262,144 transitions per (shape, label) pair in one packed comparison. The source-only reproduction `verify_strengthened.py --extra-chains K6R3C7W` (`outputs/strengthened-direction-c7w-reproduction-manifest.json`, 283 steps, the 238 and 235 chains reused from the same build directory) checked it in about 15 minutes on the loaded machine: data module 682 s (32 MB of chunked numerals, elaboration heartbeat limit lifted), 34 shards 206 s in total, table 30 s, sequence 10 s; every bound audits to `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`. The independent judge is re-deciding the new certificates (`data/judge_direction_k6c7.json` when finished).

### Toward 232: a two-level oracle

For 232 the mean must reach about 4.5 (with the potential range seen so far). The cycles that stop the cap-7 refinement contain no lumped 8s (for example `5,5,3,3,6,4,5,3,4,4`), so a higher cap does not help; their windows are feasible in the radius-three ball. Deciding such windows again in the radius-four ball (`refine.TwoLevelOracle`, `refine_fast.py --escalate 4`) refutes some of them: 9 of 60 feasible windows on the minimum cycles at mean 48/11, mostly in under two seconds. Both balls encode only necessary conditions, so a refutation at either radius excludes the window; the minimiser records the refuting radius and the certifier uses it. Measured per unit of compute, querying every window of every extracted cycle beats breaking each cycle with as few queries as possible (`--greedy`): a refuted window also removes edges far from the cycle it was asked for. The default is therefore `--hybrid`: all windows at radius three, then greedy radius-four escalation for cycles still unbroken. Query time is dominated by a tail (5% of the windows took 71% of the time), and CaDiCaL cannot be interrupted through pysat, so each query runs in its own process started from a forkserver and is killed at the limit (`refine.run_limited`, 10 s); killed windows count as feasible, which also keeps the later certificates cheap. Oracle answers are cached per radius and written as they arrive; one round costs about two minutes of automaton work (graph 10 s, Howard 6 s, cycle extraction 120 s) plus the queries.

## Start-state accounting: conditional bound 232

The refinement reached mean 9/2 at cap 7 (`data/refine_L7_k6_K7_r3.log`, round 3 of the hybrid two-level run), and the exact walk bound of the cached exclusions is then 232. The potential bound was not: its constant `L P - Q (L - 1) + R` charges the full potential range `R` to the start, and the smallest range at 9/2 (bounded Bellman-Ford with a virtual source and binary search over the range, `scripts/min_range.py`) is 15, which gives 233. The range is the wrong quantity. The sequence lemma only needs the start state's potential, and the start state carries its own `k - 1` symbols, which also count towards `sum alpha`. So the constant becomes `L P - Q (L - k) + D` with `D = max over states (pi - Q * symbol sum)` and `pi >= 0`; minimising `D` is again a bounded Bellman-Ford, with per-state upper bounds `D + Q * symbol sum` (`min_range.min_start_potential`). At 9/2 the best potential has `D = -2` (range 64, which still fits the 8-bit fields: `P + R <= 128`, `8 Q + R < 128`), and the bound is **232**. `minimize_fast.py --start --escalate 4 --lam 9/2` keeps 15,959 windows for this potential (`data/minimal_L7_k6_K7_r3_r34.json`; 7,822 of them already certified, 84 of the new ones refuted only at radius four), `refine_fast.py --index --start` re-derives `D` from a certificate index and checks the potential on every edge, and `gen_lean_swar.py --start` proves the start bound in Lean with one packed comparison per shape against a table of symbol sums (`field_le_of_swarOK`). All 15,959 windows are drat-trim-certified (`data/certify_direction_r34.log`; the index `data/direction_certificates_k6.json` now holds 16,366 verified cases and no failure). `refine_fast.py --index --start` rebuilds the automaton from the certified windows alone: minimum mean 9/2, exact walk bound 232 edges, and a start potential with `D = -2` checked on all 20,075,592 edges (`data/direction_bound_L7_k6_K7_r34.json`). The Lean chain `WindowAutomatonK6R34C7W*` (generated from that index, `gen_lean_swar.py --start`) proves `upper_232_of_direction_windowsK6R34C7W`: **at most 232 edges** if none of the 15,959 certified window patterns occurs. The reproduction `outputs/strengthened-direction-232-reproduction-manifest.json` (323 steps, success, both cap-7 chains, `--jobs 4`) checked the new chain in 390 module-seconds (data module 110 s, table 20 s, wrapper 79 s with its mathlib import) and 4.6 minutes of wall time for the whole run; every bound audits to `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`. Windows that needed lazy cycle cuts took 77% of its time (a solve-and-cut loop, then a cold re-proof for a clean DRUP proof), and the cuts are almost all 8-cycles of the same few balls. A cut forbids one cycle of the ball from being entirely selected, and a snake induces a path, so every cut is valid for every window with the same direction shape; `certify_direction.py` now adds the cuts already found for a shape before the first solve (`data/cycle_cuts.json`, shared across restarts). After the restart only 8 of the first 175 windows needed a new cut, the mean time per window fell from 11 s to 6.3 s, and throughput on four cores rose from about 20 to 35 windows a minute.

### Smaller proofs at cap 7

Profiling the cap-7 data module (682 s) showed 544 s in the 34 layout-consistency loops: `potT` stores each shape's potentials with the three newest base-8 digits of the code rotated, and `consT` compared the two layouts field by field (32,768 kernel iterations, about 16 s per shape). The rotation of the nine low index bits of every field is a product of six transpositions of index bits, and each is one delta swap `x ^^^ t ^^^ (t <<< s)` with `t = ((x >>> s) ^^^ x) &&& m`, a handful of big-number operations. `work/spectral/stronger/WindowAutomatonTranspose.lean` proves once that a delta swap with a byte-uniform mask disjoint from its shift moves whole fields (`testBit_dsw`, `digit_dsw`), builds the masks as products of geometric series, and composes the six swaps (`digit_trans8`: `potT = trans8 n potC` gives `digit potT (pos8 y) = digit potC y` for every `y < n`). Each shape is then one kernel equality of two 262,144-bit numbers (about 0.3 s). Two further changes remove most of the rest: large table values became separate constants, so the by-shape lemmas that unfold the lookup no longer walk megabytes of numerals, and `potR` (every 64-field block of `potC` repeated eight times) is written as the blocks placed 512 fields apart times the replicator (`ofChunksW`), eight times less text; `potR_cons` still checks it against `potC`. The regenerated `K6R3C7W` data module is 11 MB instead of 32 MB and builds in about 75 s instead of 682 s on the loaded machine; a scratch build of the whole chain (data, spec, 34 shards, table, sequence, wrapper) takes about four minutes. The reproduction `outputs/strengthened-direction-c7w-delta-reproduction-manifest.json` (284 steps, success, every bound audited to `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`) rebuilt the three SWAR chains from their sources: 117 s for the 238 chain, 181 s for the 235 chain and 230 s for the 233 chain, one module at a time on the loaded machine. It is also the first run with a dependency-aware cache: `verify_strengthened.py` used to reuse a module's object whenever the module's own source was unchanged, even after a module it imports had been rebuilt with changed definitions (here the shards after the regenerated data module), and now reuses an object only if it is newer than every object it imports. `--jobs N` checks up to `N` independent modules at once (a module starts when everything it imports is checked; the 34 shards of a chain are independent).

## Lower bound

A delegated check found no longer snake. The 131 published 191-edge snakes (Orland et al., data repository Math-AI-Caltech/Snake-in-the-Box) pass an independent checker and are exactly `work/bank9.json`; the 1,042 published 190-edge snakes and the published 192-vertex induced cycle also pass (a 194-vertex cycle would give 192 edges by `CoilReduction.lean`). Exhaustive regrowth from the cut-open cycle (128, 120 and partly 112 vertices kept), 32-vertex replacements inside it, 39 million combined replace-and-regrow moves, and regrowth of every 190-edge snake with 128 vertices kept found nothing longer than 191 and only rediscovered known 191-edge snakes. The remaining open gap in the upper-bound programme is the geometric bridge (above), which would make the conditional bounds unconditional.

## Ceilings of count-only window methods

Explicit adversaries (found by the collaborating local session and re-checked here with `scripts/wincert.py`) bound what any automaton over lumped singleton counts can prove:

- `Q9 = Q5 × Q4` with two disjoint induced 14-cycles in `Q5` placed on even and odd fibres gives an induced 2-regular set of 224 vertices with counts 2 (×64), 5 (×128), 7 (×32); all its 2-, 3- and 4-centre windows are feasible at radius three, so no radius-three automaton with at most four centres can exceed mean 30/7 (about 233 edges). One 5-centre window at radius three refutes it.
- `Q9 = Q6 × Q3` with two disjoint induced 26-cycles in `Q6` gives 208 vertices with lumped mean 69/13; all windows with at most five centres are feasible at radius three and all with at most four centres at radius four, so those routes stop near 227 edges. Six-centre windows at radius three are the first lever that can see it.
- Real 191-edge snakes have lumped mean `min(alpha,6)` about 5.92 and true mean `alpha` about 8.7, so a lumped-at-six method cannot pass about 224 edges at any radius and any count-only method cannot pass about 209.
- Charging each empty square `2/21` to each selected vertex adjacent to it (a square has 28 neighbours forming seven disjoint squares, each holding at most three selected vertices) is computable from the radius-three ball (`Encoding(..., empties=...)`), but the count pattern alone forces almost nothing about it there: `e(mid) <= 4` stays feasible for patterns such as (2,5,7) or (5,5,5), because selected vertices at distance two and three (free at the boundary of the ball) kill the adjacent squares. With that charging the adversaries sit at 225.7 and 212.0 edges and real snakes at 199, so the lever only pays as a joint `(alpha, e)` state at radius four.

The six-centre refinement terminated with a locally feasible cycle of mean 4 containing six lumped-six counts, so the cheapest next lever is a finer lump cap at five or six centres (`refine_fast.py --K 7`, symbols 1..7 and `>= 8`; cached range answers imply the finer exclusions).

## What is not established

- **The geometric bridge.** A snake with such a window can be normalized so that the window has a canonical shape, by a translation and a coordinate permutation. Its intersection with the ball then satisfies every clause. That argument is short and standard, but it is not formalized here. The same gap exists for the earlier radius-three (2,2,3) certificates in `milestones/07-window-223/`.
- **DRAT is not Lean.** `drat-trim` is an independent, widely used checker, but it is not part of the Lean kernel. The CNF generator is also unverified, though the positive controls exercise it. Converting these certificates into Lean, as was attempted for (2,2,3), remains open.
- **Beyond 241.** The shape-blind four-window ceiling is 3, so further progress here needs more information:
  - *Direction-aware states.* Feasible low quadruples such as `(3,3,3,3)` occur only in shape `0,1,2,3,0`. Three consecutive windows of that shape would make `x_7` adjacent to `x_0`, so an automaton that also tracks directions should exceed mean 3. `scripts/direction_automaton.py` does this: it tracks the last seven directions and checks each window against the survey for its own shape. Its minimum mean is about **10/3**, found by binary search. With small constants that would give roughly 239–240 edges. It relies on many more solver-only exclusions, none certified yet.
  - *Radius four.*
  - *The empty-square term `s0`, which every current bound discards.* A CP-SAT probe at radius four found that a count-one vertex forces at least four empty squares meeting its neighbours. At radius three nothing is forced. This is uncertified.
